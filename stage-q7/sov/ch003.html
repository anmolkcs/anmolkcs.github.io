The iron cage was forged from thumb-thick bars of cold iron, yet three of the middle bars were bent outward at a frightening angle, stained with frozen frost and dark blood.

Coiled inside the cage was the headless corpse of a Second-Order Spirit Beast: a Crimson-Scaled Python, nearly thirty feet long and as thick as a water barrel. Even with its skull cleaved off by a Houtian master’s blade hours ago, the overlapping scarlet scales along its back still pulsed with a faint, suffocating heat, and its severed neck muscles twitched sporadically on the flagstones.

A Second-Order spirit beast was the equivalent of a human martial artist at the Fourth to Sixth Stage of Body Transformation. In the wilderness outside Cloudstride City, a single adult Crimson-Scaled Python could slaughter an entire squad of armored city guards.

“Listen up!” Steward Zhao hurried into the third kitchen yard, his fat face glistening with sweat as he waved a horsehair whisk. “Lord城主 (City Lord) Ouyang’s younger brother is hosting a banquet on the third floor tonight for an envoy from the Seven Star Martial House! The main course is the marrow-tendon and heart-blood soup of this Second-Order Crimson-Scaled Python. Master Liu, we need the main dorsal tendon and the intact blood-sac extracted within two quarters of an hour!”

Master Liu’s expression turned solemn as he examined the massive python carcass. “Steward Zhao, a Second-Order Crimson-Scaled Python’s dorsal tendon is wrapped inside thirty-six interlocking ring-bones near the heart cavity. My right hand caught frost-poison from an Ice-Toad last winter; if my fingers stiffen halfway through the heart cavity, I’ll pierce the secondary venom gland right beside the blood-sac.”

Steward Zhao’s face went pale. “If the venom gland leaks a single drop into the heart-blood, the banquet on the third floor is ruined and both our heads will roll! Who else in the yard has steady enough knife-work for fine bone-stripping?”

Zhao Hu instinctively shrank back half a step. Brute strength could hack sheep bones, but a Second-Order python’s ring-bones were narrower than a pinky finger and slick with corrosive lymph; one slip meant losing both hands to rot-poison and getting flogged to death by the Pavilion guards.

Master Liu turned his head slowly and looked at the quiet fifteen-year-old standing beside the third workbench.

Over the past six days, Lu Chen had deboned thirty-eight Iron-Backed Flood Fish without rupturing a single paper-thin bitter-gall sac. His thirty-two-jin knife moved with a terrifying, machine-like precision that made even Master Liu’s scalp tingle.

“Little Chen,” Master Liu said, his voice low and grave. “Dare to take the knife while I guide the bone joints? If we deliver the heart-blood sac intact, the Pavilion rewards two whole taels of silver—equivalent to two hundred copper coins.”

Two taels of silver was more than six days of grueling labor combined, and enough to buy three doses of Blood-Boiling Powder from the apothecary.

Lu Chen stepped forward without hesitation. “Please guide me, Master Liu.”

“Good lad!” Master Liu clapped his shoulder, grabbed a pair of iron hooks to pry open the python’s chest plates, and barked, “Everyone else, step back ten paces and hold the tallow torches high!”

As the thick scarlet ventral scales were pried apart, a wave of scorching, metallic heat billowed out from the python’s chest cavity.

Unlike First-Order beasts whose blood cooled rapidly after death, the heart-blood of a Second-Order Crimson-Scaled Python remained near boiling temperature for twelve hours, brimming with fierce Yang-attribute blood essence.

“Thirty-six ring-bones, spaced half an inch apart,” Master Liu pointed with a bronze needle. “Slide the tip of your knife under the left cartilage ridge, angle fifteen degrees inward, and sever the suspensory fibers one by one. Do not touch the purple sac beneath the third rib!”

Lu Chen inhaled deeply and sank his consciousness into Prism Stillness.

The clamoring voices, Steward Zhao’s nervous pacing, and the suffocating heat of the python’s carcass vanished. In Lu Chen’s eyes, only the labyrinth of ivory ring-bones, crimson blood vessels, and the pulsing dark-red heart-blood sac remained.

Shhk.

The thirty-two-jin black-iron knife entered the cavity.

Because a Second-Order beast’s fascia was ten times tougher than a First-Order boar’s, every millimeter of forward progress felt like carving through seasoned ironwood. Sweat poured down Lu Chen’s temples, stinging his eyes, yet he didn’t blink once. One ring-bone, five ring-bones, twenty ring-bones—his wrist maintained the exact same tension from the first stroke to the thirtieth.

At the thirty-sixth ring-bone, as Lu Chen severed the final anchoring tendon beneath the python’s heart-blood sac, a pressurized capillary on the outer wall of the heart-vein spurted a single droplet of thick, molten-gold crimson blood—the purest Core Blood Essence of the Second-Order python!

The droplet splashed directly onto the back of Lu Chen’s right hand, right over an old blister that had split open during the cut.

Hiss!

The drop of Second-Order blood essence burned like liquid magma. Before it could scorch his flesh, however, an invisible suction force erupted from the center of Lu Chen’s chest.

Thrum—!

Through the meridians of his right arm, the drop of Crimson-Scaled Python blood essence was drawn straight into his spiritual sea and swallowed whole by the nine-faceted Chrono-Soul Prism!

Outwardly, Lu Chen didn’t flinch. With iron composure, he lifted the intact, football-sized heart-blood sac and the shimmering silver dorsal tendon onto Master Liu’s jade platter, wiped his blade, and accepted the two heavy taels of silver from a wildly relieved Steward Zhao.

When Lu Chen returned to his family’s small tavern courtyard in the southern quarter late that night, a warm yellow oil lamp was still burning behind the paper lattice window of the main room.

Before his hand even touched the wooden latch, the door creaked open, and a gentle woman in her late thirties wearing a patched indigo apron hurried out with a wool cloak in her hands. That was his mother, Meng Wan. Behind her stood his father, Lu Shan—a broad-shouldered man whose left knee had carried a stiff limp ever since a beast-tide patrol injury ten years ago, holding a steaming clay pot of mutton-and-radish soup on a wooden tray.

“Chen’er, why are you back so late tonight?” Meng Wan draped the warm cloak over his shoulders, her gentle brows knitting with heartache as she took his rough, blister-calloused right hand between her palms. “Master Liu kept you at the chopping block until the night watch again? Come sit by the stove—your father simmered this mutton soup with two slices of old mountain ginseng for four hours so it wouldn’t lose its heat.”

“I’m not tired at all, Mother. Look—Master Liu gave me a two-tael silver bonus tonight for stripping a Second-Order Python’s tendon cleanly,” Lu Chen smiled warmly, pressing one of the heavy silver taels into his mother’s hand while sitting down at the scrubbed pine table. Watching his father pour him a bowl of milky-white mutton soup piled high with meat while pouring only clear radish broth into his own bowl, Lu Chen felt a deep, quiet warmth fill his chest.

On Earth, he had been an orphan since college; in this vast, unfamiliar world, the unconditional love of Lu Shan and Meng Wan was the most precious treasure he had been given. Secretly, he picked up three thick pieces of mutton with his chopsticks and placed them into his father’s and mother’s bowls.

“Father, Mother, eat with me. Starting next week, you won’t have to worry about Wang Yigao or the tavern’s lease anymore—and I’ll find a way to cure Father’s old knee injury so both of you can cultivate alongside me for a hundred years.”

Lu Shan laughed warmly, ruffling Lu Chen’s hair with his rough palm. “Just hearing you say that makes my knee feel half-healed already! Eat up and get some sleep, son.”

Half an hour later, after making sure his parents were resting peacefully and bolting the wooden door of his side-shed, Lu Chen sat cross-legged on his plank bed and pulled his consciousness into his spiritual sea.

Inside the dark expanse of his mind, the Chrono-Soul Prism was no longer dull ash-gray.

Fed by the concentrated blood essence of the Second-Order Crimson-Scaled Python, the first of the nine facets glowed with a faint, ancient starlight, pulling Lu Chen’s spiritual sense inside the Prism itself.

Lu Chen found himself standing in a boundless, mist-shrouded void where time stood completely still.

Directly ahead, at the unreachable center of the Prism’s inner cosmos, floated a nine-petaled lotus formed by the Prism’s own primordial time-light. Resting within the petals in deep, motionless hibernation was the soul of a young woman in snow-white robes.

As Lu Chen’sspiritual sense brushed the outer mist, a brief, wordless visual echo recorded by the Prism itself flashed across his mind like a silent reflection on water:

He saw that this nine-faceted gray Prism had been an ancient ancestral heirloom carried by the snow-robed woman—yet despite trying every method in her lifetime, even she had never been able to unlock or activate it. Then, in a catastrophic battle where she was surrounded and outnumbered by shadowy enemies in the void, she had chosen to self-destruct rather than surrender. At the exact instant of her self-destruction, the Chrono-Soul Prism had awakened on its own, unleashing a vortex that pulled her soul—along with the souls of all the powerhouses who perished around her—into its core before tearing through space-time!

For some mysterious reason held deep within the Prism’s laws, the Prism had preserved the snow-robed woman’s soul intact inside the central lotus, while its merciless grinding laws had automatically stripped and crushed all the other absorbed souls—erasing every trace of their personal identities, names, memories, and world knowledge, and distilling them into pure, anonymous Technique Memory Bubbles!

Hovering in concentric rings around the outer facets of the Prism were hundreds of these pure Technique Memory Bubbles—some blindingly gold near the center, and others near the outermost edge glowing in pale crimson or silver.

All except one of the outermost bubbles were still locked behind an invisible film of gray frost.

Only the smallest pale-crimson Technique Bubble at the very edge of the first facet—unlocked by the drop of Second-Order Python blood essence—was drifting slowly toward Lu Chen’s spiritual avatar.

Taking a deep breath, Lu Chen braced his mind and reached out his hand to touch the pale-crimson Technique Bubble.
