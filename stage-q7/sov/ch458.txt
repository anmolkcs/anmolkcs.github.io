<p>Inside the smoky smithy shed, all eyes turned from the wounded Mud-Well survivor to <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong>, <strong>Uncle Shi</strong>, and <strong>Lu Chen</strong>.</p>
<p>For a long moment, Old Tie Shan stood motionless beside the stack of eighty gleaming ten-jin bars of Cold-Slag Iron that Lu Chen had bled and hammered for twenty-five days to forge. The sixty-year-old blacksmith looked at Hunter Feng's mangled arm, then at fourteen-year-old <strong>Shi Tou</strong>, <strong>Big-Tall Zhao</strong>, <strong>Old Ma</strong>, and finally at Lu Chen.</p>
<p>Slowly, Old Tie Shan reached inside his leather apron, pulled out the broken half of the iron mine-tally marked <strong>"Seven"</strong>, and laid it down on the cold anvil.</p>
<p>"Lu Chen... Uncle Shi," Old Tie Shan said, his voice hoarse and trembling as he looked at the village men. "One-Eyed Wolf Han Wu is a <strong>Peak Tier Three (Bowstring-Sinew Perfection)</strong> killer who served ten years in the Grey-Wall Frontier Heavy Infantry before deserting. He's got eighteen hardened outlaws holding a stone fort inside a fifty-foot rock pass. You already risked your lives in the eastern basin and at the charcoal kilns for my boy. I can't ask the men of Ash-Brook to walk into a stone choke-point against eighteen bandits just to save my Tie Niu... Let me take one hand-sled alone down the pass tomorrow. If Han Wu wants a head to pay for Scar-Six, he can take mine—"</p>
<p>"Shut your mouth, Old Tie!"</p>
<p>Uncle Shi stepped forward and slammed the butt of his new iron-pine spear onto the cinder floor so hard the slate tiles jumped.</p>
<p>"Did you forget why Han Wu is squatting in Howling-Wind Pass?!" Uncle Shi barked fiercely. "Even if your boy Tie Niu wasn't down in Pit #7, if Ash-Brook doesn't deliver its winter tax to the Black-Rock Tally-House before the twentieth of the twelfth month, <strong>Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan</strong>—who is in league with Han Wu!—will ride up here on the twenty-first with garrison troops, seize our anvil, take our sons to the mines, and leave our women and children to starve in the snow! And what about the families of Mud-Well Village hiding in the freezing ridge caves right now without a grain of millet?! Walking into Howling-Wind Pass isn't just for Tie Niu—it's the only road where Ash-Brook and Mud-Well live to see the spring!"</p>
<p>"Uncle Shi is right," <strong>Big-Tall Zhao</strong> growled, slapping his broad chest as <strong>Cross-Eyed Liu</strong> and <strong>Old Ma</strong> stepped up beside him. "When Old Tie sharpened our hoes and patched our pots for thirty winters without charging a copper to a hungry family, nobody called it charity! And with Lu Chen leading our spears, when have we ever lost a fight to Broken-Tooth Camp?!"</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan's single eye reddened as he looked around the circle of rough, weathered faces.</p>
<p>Then everyone looked toward Lu Chen, who had been studying a charcoal sketch of <strong>Howling-Wind Pass</strong> that Uncle Shi had scratched onto a flat slab of slate.</p>
<p>"Howling-Wind Pass is three miles long, flanked by vertical shale cliffs a hundred feet high, with the ruined stone watch-fort sitting at the narrowest fifty-foot bend in the middle," Lu Chen said quietly, tapping the center of the slate map with his fingernail. "Han Wu expects us either to cower in the village until the deadline expires, or to march meekly up the road in daylight and get pinned against his ice-barricade by arrows while he charges down the slope."</p>
<p>Lu Chen looked up at the dark clouds massing over the peaks of the Black-Briar Ridge.</p>
<p>"In two days—on the first of the twelfth month—the three-day <strong>Great Solstice Blizzard</strong> hits the pass. During the whiteout, visibility drops to five paces, and the gale screams through the gorge loud enough to drown out rolling boulders. We don't wait for the twentieth. We leave at dawn on the first day of the blizzard with the two iron-clad sledges and twelve men—and we break Han Wu's spine in the storm."</p>
