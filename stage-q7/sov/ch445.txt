<p>Bitter-Pine Ravine was a narrow, V-shaped gash in the western flank of the Black-Briar Barren Ridge where dense groves of century-old <strong>Bitter-Iron Pines</strong> grew rooted directly into cracks of black basalt.</p>
<p>Because these trees drew subterranean iron-water and sulfur through their roots under tenfold gravity, their dark-red timber was nearly as hard and heavy as seasoned bone. When a woodsman struck a mature Bitter-Iron Pine with an ordinary axe, the blade would bounce off with a metallic <em>clang</em> and leave barely a white scratch in the bark; yet once slow-baked for six days inside an oxygen-starved earthen kiln, that same iron-hard timber turned into <strong>Black-Gold Pine Charcoal</strong>—a smokeless fuel that rang like clinking coins when dropped and burned hot enough to melt cold-slag steel.</p>
<p>Arriving in the snow-choked ravine before noon on the eleventh day of the eleventh month, the five-man crew immediately cleared the snowdrifts from the seven beehive-shaped <strong>Earthen Charcoal Kilns</strong> built into the western dirt bank.</p>
<p>Then came the backbreaking work of felling and sectioning twenty thousand jin of green Bitter-Iron Pine timber to pack all seven kilns before nightfall.</p>
<p>While Uncle Shi, Big-Tall Zhao, and Cross-Eyed Liu worked in pairs with two-man crosscut saws on the smaller trees, Lu Chen walked alone up the steep basalt slope to the oldest, thickest trunks—trees two feet in diameter whose wood was dense enough to dull a normal saw in ten minutes.</p>
<p>In his hands, Lu Chen held a custom <strong>thirty-six-pound Cold-Slag Felling Axe</strong> he had forged himself before leaving the village.</p>
<p>Planting his boots knee-deep in the snow on the forty-degree slope, Lu Chen sank his mind into <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>.</p>
<p>Within his three-pace perception, he did not swing blindly at the iron-hard bark. Instead, he read the winter frost-tension lines running through the spiral grain of the trunk, locked his compacted <strong>Early Tier Two</strong> muscles from ankle to shoulder, and whipped the thirty-six-pound axe horizontally in a series of explosive, waist-driven <strong>Hip-Snap Hewing Strikes</strong>!</p>
<p><em>THWACK—BOOM!</em> <em>THWACK—BOOM!</em></p>
<p>Every blow carried over two thousand two hundred jin of focused cutting force and landed within a tenth of an inch of the previous notch! Reddish-brown wood chips the size of dinner plates flew twenty feet across the snow. With each jarring impact against the bone-hard iron-pine trunk, the shockwave traveled back through the axe helve into the deep, hard-to-reach <strong>erector spinae, serratus, and oblique muscle layers</strong> wrapping Lu Chen's spine and ribs—wringing out the last traces of deep-seated mortal water and compacting his core torso muscles into <strong>Middle Tier Two of the Mortal-Shedding Stage (2,300 jin baseline force)</strong>!</p>
<p>In four hours of relentless work that left his bare bronze torso steaming like a boiler in the sub-zero gale, Lu Chen felled and bucked fourteen massive Bitter-Iron Pines into four-foot billets, hauling them down the slope two thousand pounds at a time on a rope-sled!</p>
<p>By twilight, all seven beehive kilns were packed tight to their domes with twenty thousand jin of iron-pine billets, sealed over with wet yellow clay and ash-mud, and ignited from their central firing-channels.</p>
<p>As night fell over the freezing ravine, seven thin columns of thick, milky-white steam began to curl up from the clay side-vents of the kilns—the first stage of "sweating the water" out of the wood.</p>
