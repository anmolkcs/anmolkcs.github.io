<p>Drift Four was a half-mile-long subterranean purgatory carved straight into the living belly of the Earth-Marrow Iron fault.</p>
<p>The ceiling of jagged black granite sloped so low in places that a tall man had to walk slightly stooped, while ankle-deep, scalding <strong>Sulfur-Iron Brine</strong>—hundred-and-ten-degree mineral water seeping out of the deep volcanic fissures—flowed sluggishly along the tunnel floor, filling the air with a suffocating stench of rotten eggs, blood, and wet iron rust.</p>
<p>Every thirty paces along the dripping walls, gaunt, skeletal men clad only in shredded hemp loincloths swung heavy thirty-pound iron pickaxes against the glittering black rock face.</p>
<p>Under fifteenfold gravity, the thirty-pound iron leg-shackle riveted around each miner's ankle weighed as much as a four-hundred-and-fifty-pound ball-and-chain in the lower worlds. Their skin was blistered and peeled by the scalding sulfur-brine; their ribs jutted out like basket-work beneath layers of coal-soot; and their hollow eyes barely blinked as Lu Chen's boots splashed past them in the dark.</p>
<p>Whenever a miner slowed his swing to cough up a mouthful of gray ash-phlegm, one of the six <strong>Tier-Two Iron-Pick Wardens</strong> patrolling the drift would lash out with a knotted rawhide whip braided with iron wire, drawing a stripe of blood across the man's back.</p>
<p>Lu Chen walked the entire half-mile length of Drift Four in silence, his heart growing colder and heavier with every hundred paces, while his three-pace <strong>Prism Stillness</strong> scanned the faces and breathing rhythms of the thirty-nine starving men along the rock wall.</p>
<p>At the very end of the half-mile drift, where the tunnel widened into a steaming, dead-end cul-de-sac right over a bubbling fissure of hot sulfur-brine, the air was so thin and scorching that even the patrolling wardens stayed twenty paces back near the ventilation pipe.</p>
<p>There, working alone at the hardest, wettest corner of the black iron-vein by the dim green glow of a single dying moss-lamp, stood the fortieth miner.</p>
<p>He was a broad-shouldered, once-burly young man of twenty-two whose frame carried the exact same wide-boned build as <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong>. His face was Gaunt and covered in a matted black beard; across his back ran a dozen crisscrossing whip-scars, some old and white, others fresh and oozing.</p>
<p>Worst of all was his left leg.</p>
<p>A heavy thirty-pound iron shackle was riveted directly over his left ankle, which had been crushed three weeks ago by a runaway ore-cart and never set properly—leaving the ankle joint swollen to the size of a melon and twisted inward at a hideous thirty-degree angle. Every time the young man braced his weight under the fifteenfold gravity to swing his thirty-pound pickaxe at the rock wall, his crushed left ankle ground bone-on-bone in the scalding sulfur-brine, making his whole body convulse with silent agony and cold sweat pour down his chin.</p>
<p>Yet he did not stop swinging.</p>
<p>And with every hoarse, rattling breath between swings of the pickaxe, the young man whispered the same words under his breath to keep himself from collapsing into the brine:</p>
<p><em>"Sixty-four carts left... hold on, leg... My old man only has one arm left in the snow... if I die down here, nobody pulls his bellows..."</em></p>
<p>Twenty paces back by the ventilation pipe, a scar-necked <strong>Early Tier Two</strong> warden named <strong>Ma Gui</strong> spat his betel-nut into the water and raised his iron-wired whip as the crippled young man stumbled to one knee against the rock face.</p>
<p>"Up on your feet, #704! That's the third time you dropped to your knee this hour! Give me five more carts before the night-bell, or I'll break your right ankle to match the left!"</p>
<p><em>CRACK—!</em></p>
<p>Warden Ma Gui lashed the twelve-foot iron-wired whip straight at the crippled young man's bare, scarred neck!</p>
<p>Before the barbed iron tip could touch a single hair on the young man's skin, a matte-bronze hand reached out of the darkness behind the lamp and caught the flying iron-wired whip cleanly in midair!</p>
