<p>The fifteenth morning of the tenth autumn month dawned clear, brittle, and brutally cold.</p>
<p>Shortly before midday, while the seventy-three residents of Ash-Brook Village stood gathered in the frozen mud square behind the two loaded tribute sledges, the deep, brazen blare of a <strong>Bronze Bailiff Horn</strong> echoed down from the southern gorge.</p>
<p><em>WUUUUUUUU—!</em></p>
<p>Even from half a mile away, the sound carried a heavy, metallic tremor that made the loose slate tiles on the village roofs rattle and the children whimper against their mothers' skirts. Moments later, the heavy, rhythmic thunder of iron-shod hooves struck the frozen gravel road outside the southern log-gate.</p>
<p>Six riders trotted through the open palisade into the narrow village square.</p>
<p>They did not ride ordinary horses—for ordinary horses would break their legs within a month under the frontier's tenfold Desolate Gravity. Instead, they rode <strong>Scale-Hoofed Crag-Mules</strong>, muscular, short-necked beasts the height of draft stallions, covered in coarse slate-gray hair with overlapping iron-hard scales from knee to hoof and pairs of curved bone spurs jutting from their lower jaws.</p>
<p>Five of the riders wore black leather brigandines studded with iron rivets, carrying heavy crossbows and notched ring-pommel sabers at their hips; every one of them radiated the compact, rough-skinned physical presence of <strong>Tier Two (Iron-Flesh Compacting)</strong> or <strong>Early Tier Three (Bowstring-Sinew Drawing)</strong> Mortal-Shedding veterans.</p>
<p>At the head of the column rode <strong>Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan</strong>.</p>
<p>He was a square-jawed, thick-necked man in his late thirties wearing a half-coat oflinked black iron mail over a wolf-skin collar. Across his saddle-bow rested a six-foot <strong>Iron-Ringed Whip-Rod</strong> weighing nearly ninety jin, and when his booted foot rested in the iron stirrup, the wooden crossbeam of the stirrup groaned under his density.</p>
<p>From twenty paces back under the shadowy eaves of the smithy shed, standing motionless beside the ox-hide bellows, Lu Chen observed Zhao Kuan through narrowed eyes.</p>
<p>Even without expanding <em>Prism Stillness</em> beyond three paces, Lu Chen could read the physical markers of the man's cultivation from the way the cold wind parted around him: Zhao Kuan's skin was a dull, weather-cured bronze (<strong>Tier 1</strong>), the muscles of his neck and forearms were condensed into tight, waterless ridges (<strong>Tier 2</strong>), the tendons along his wrists snapped faintly like taut bowstrings whenever he shifted his reins (<strong>Tier 3</strong>), and most tellingly, when he dismounted from his Crag-Mule onto the frozen mud square, his boots sank two inches into the permafrost with a dull, metallic thud—the unmistakable weight of <strong>Early Tier Four of the Mortal-Shedding Stage: Slag-Bone Calcining</strong>!</p>
<p>In his previous life as a Primordial Sovereign, Lu Chen wouldn't have spared a glance for a mortal warrior whose bones were merely hardened to the density of slag-iron.</p>
<p>Yet here in Ash-Brook Village—where the strongest hunter, <strong>Uncle Shi</strong>, was only an aging <strong>Peak Tier One / Half-Step Tier Two</strong> whose joints were stiff with ash-damp, and where Lu Chen himself had just stepped into <strong>Early Tier One</strong> two nights ago—an Early Tier Four warrior with calcined iron bones and a ninety-jin whip-rod was a walking siege engine. A single backhand from Zhao Kuan's iron-boned fist carried over four thousand jin of percussive force, enough to cave in an unarmored mortal's ribcage like an eggshell.</p>
<p>"Village Head! Old Tie!" Zhao Kuan's voice boomed across the silent square as he slapped his leather riding gloves against his thigh, blowing a cloud of white steam into the freezing air. "The County Magistrate's winter border-levy came down from <strong>Grey-Wall Prefecture</strong> three days ago! The northern Bone-Totem tribes are raiding the prefectural trenches early this year, so every village in Black-Rock County has had its iron quality standard raised by one grade! Let's see what kind of garbage your one-armed forge turned out this month!"</p>
