<p>Uncle Shi—acting as village head since the old elder died last winter—and Old Tie Shan stepped forward to the two tribute sledges, bowing low in the frozen mud as two bailiff guards pulled the twenty-eight newly forged mattocks and three hundred jin of pig-iron bars onto the stone inspection slab.</p>
<p>"Lord Sub-Bailiff," Uncle Shi said respectfully, holding up the carved wooden tally-board with his three-fingered left hand and his right palm. "Twenty-eight Black-Marrow mattocks, three hundred jin of smelted iron, twelve cured beast hides, and forty jin of dried Ash-Root—every ounce weighed on the standard stone balance."</p>
<p>Zhao Kuan didn't even glance at the wooden tally-board. Stepping up to the stone slab, he picked up three of the twenty-eight mattocks at random, turned them upside down, and slammed their flat iron blades edge-first with all his <strong>Early Tier Four</strong> strength against the granite testing-boulder beside the village well!</p>
<p><em>CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!</em></p>
<p>Three clear, bell-like rings echoed across the square. Sparks flew from the granite boulder, deep white gouges appeared in the hard stone—and not a single one of the three mattock blades chipped, bent, or cracked at the eye-socket!</p>
<p>Zhao Kuan's bushy eyebrows twitched in surprise. He picked up three more mattocks from the bottom of the pile and smashed them even harder against the granite boulder.</p>
<p><em>CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!</em></p>
<p>Again, the dense <strong>Black-Marrow Iron</strong>, forged by Lu Chen's forty-pound sledge along the spiral slag-expulsion lines, rang like fine temple bronze without a single cold-shatter fracture!</p>
<p>A flicker of irritation crossed Zhao Kuan's square face. Clearly, he had ridden up to Ash-Brook expecting at least four or five of the one-armed blacksmith's mattocks to snap during testing—giving him an easy excuse to levy a five-hundred-jin default fine and pocket the penalty silver.</p>
<p>"Hmph. Looks like you dug into a lucky pocket of marrow-ore in the gully," Zhao Kuan muttered coldly, tossing the sixth mattock back onto the sledge. He turned his horsehide boot to leave, then Old Tie Shan took a trembling half-step forward in the mud and dropped to both knees before the Sub-Bailiff's stirrup.</p>
<p>"Lord Zhao!" Old Tie Shan pleaded, pressing his forehead nearly to the frozen ruts while holding up a small cloth pouch containing six polished <strong>Iron-Jaw Hyena Fangs</strong> and two ounces of dried <strong>Blood-Moss</strong>—every scrap of personal savings the old man possessed. "My boy <strong>Tie Niu</strong>... his three-year bond in <strong>Deep-Shaft Pit #7</strong> ends at the Winter Solstice in the twelfth month! Last month a sulfur-carter said Overseer <strong>Luo Shan</strong> added fifty taels of axle-debt to his tally! Lord Zhao, you drink with Overseer Luo's clerks at the tally-house—please, tell me if my boy is still breathing down in Pit #7! Tell me how much iron I have to forge this winter to buy his bond back before the spring thaw!"</p>
<p>Under the smithy eaves twenty paces away, Lu Chen's fingers tightened silently around the wooden upright of the ox-hide bellows.</p>
<p>In the center of the square, Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan looked down at the kneeling, one-armed old blacksmith, then kicked the small cloth pouch of hyena fangs out of Old Tie Shan's hand into the slush.</p>
<p>"Your son Tie Niu?" Zhao Kuan sneered, spitting a wad of chewed betel-bark onto the snow beside Old Tie's knee. "That stubborn mule broke his left ankle trying to keep an ore-cart from rolling over some half-dead miner in Drift Four last month. Overseer Luo Shan says a lame mule eats too much millet for what he hauls. Unless somebody brings <strong>eight hundred jin of Grade-One Refined Cold-Slag Iron</strong>—or <strong>thirty taels of Prefectural Tribute Silver</strong>—to the Black-Slag Canyon Tally-House before the twentieth day of the twelfth month, Shaft #7 is having its lower drifts blasted shut for the winter flooding, and the debt-miners in Drift Four are staying down there with the water!"</p>
<p>"Blasted shut?!" Old Tie Shan's face went white as ash. Lunging forward on his knees, the desperate old father grabbed the hem of Zhao Kuan's iron-mail coat with his single scarred hand. "Lord Zhao! Three years ago the bond was signed for three hundred jin of iron! We paid every ounce! You can't bury forty men alive for a broken cart-axle—!"</p>
<p>"Get your filthy coal-stained paw off my mail, old cripple!"</p>
<p>Zhao Kuan didn't even draw his whip-rod. With a casual, disgusted flick of his iron-boned right boot, the Early Tier Four Sub-Bailiff kicked Old Tie Shan squarely in the right collarbone!</p>
<p><em>KRAK!</em></p>
<p>With the sickening snap of breaking bone, Old Tie Shan's sixty-year-old frame was launched twelve feet backward across the frozen square, slamming hard into the side of the tribute sledge as a spray of bright red blood burst from his mouth!</p>
