<p>When the sun rose over the Black-Briar Barren Ridge on the twenty-eighth morning of the tenth month, the sound of that low <em>WHOOM—WHOOM—WHOOM</em> behind the smithy shed drew fourteen-year-old <strong>Shi Tou</strong>, <strong>Er Gou</strong>, and four other village youths out into the ankle-deep snow before their morning bellows shift.</p>
<p>Rounding the corner of the stone lean-to, the six boys stopped dead in their tracks.</p>
<p>In the tiny clearing behind the coal-heap, Lu Chen stood barefoot in the snow wearing only his patched hemp trousers, his matte-bronze <strong>Complete Tier One</strong> upper torso steaming faintly in the freezing air. In his hands, the seven-foot, eighty-jin solid black-iron spear moved not with flashy twirls or theatrical leaps, but with a slow, terrifyingly heavy simplicity:</p>
<p><em>Step. Thrust. Recover.</em> <em>Sweep. Coil. Thrust.</em></p>
<p>Every time his bare front heel stamped down to anchor a thrust, the frozen earth beneath two inches of snow compacted with a dull thud like a rammed-earth tamper; every time the four-edged diamond point snapped to a halt at full extension, the eighty-jin iron shaft did not quiver by the breadth of a hair—all twelve hundred jin of his bodily force transmitted cleanly into the half-inch tip!</p>
<p>"Eighty jin of solid iron..." Shi Tou breathed, clutching his own five-pound wooden hunting spear tipped with scrap hoop-iron. "Even Grandpa's thirty-pound boar-spear takes two hands just to brace against a charge, and Big Brother Lu is thrusting eighty jin one-handed on the recovery!"</p>
<p>Lu Chen completed his three-hundredth morning thrust, grounded the butt-spike of the heavy iron spear in the snow with a quiet <em>chink</em>, and looked over at the six shivering village boys.</p>
<p>"In three days, the eleventh-month hunt in the <strong>Black-Briar Woods</strong> begins," Lu Chen said, his breath rising in a steady white column. "Uncle Shi says you and Er Gou are coming along as beaters and pack-carriers this winter. Let me see how you hold those spears when a boar breaks out of the brush."</p>
<p>Eager to show off, Shi Tou and Er Gou immediately dropped into wide crouch stances in the snow, thrusting their scrap-iron wooden spears forward with loud grunts and fierce expressions.</p>
<p>Lu Chen walked over, holding nothing in his hands, and tapped the side of Shi Tou's wooden spear shaft with a single finger while pushing lightly against Er Gou's front shoulder.</p>
<p>Both fourteen-year-old boys immediately stumbled forward on their toes and sprawled face-first into the snowdrift!</p>
<p>"You're reaching with your shoulders and leaning past your knees," Lu Chen said calmly, reaching down to haul both snow-covered boys back to their feet. "In the Black-Briar Woods, the ground is frozen mud and tangled briar-roots under tenfold gravity. If you lean past your front kneecap when you thrust, and the boar slips your point by two inches, your own weight will pull you straight onto its tusks."</p>
<p>Picking up a spare ash-wood mattock handle from the woodpile, Lu Chen stepped between the six boys and sank into the foundational <strong>Three-Point Rooted Stance</strong> he had practiced since his youth in Cloudstride City—adapted specifically for the crushing downward pull of the Great Desolate Origin Universe.</p>
<p>"Don't fight the heavy ground of this ridge—use it," Lu Chen demonstrated slowly, letting the boys watch how his ankles, knees, and hips settled like three stacked millstones. "Keep sixty percent of your weight on the back heel and forty on the front ball. Your spine stays vertical like the center-post of a house. When you thrust, don't throw your chest forward; twist your back hip three inches and let the ground push the spear out through your ribs. That way, even if your spear-tip hits solid rock or misses in the fog, your feet are already rooted to step left or right."</p>
<p>For the next hour before the forge was lit, the six village youths practiced stepping and thrusting in the snow behind the smithy while Lu Chen walked down the line, adjusting an elbow here, nudging a heel there—passing on the first unadorned seeds of true spearsmanship in a forgotten corner of the Great Desolate wasteland.</p>
