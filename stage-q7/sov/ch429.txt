<p>At the fifth cock-crow on the second morning of the eleventh month, the wooden bar of Ash-Brook Village's northern palisade gate was lifted aside.</p>
<p>Six figures stepped out into the grey, freezing twilight of the Black-Frost: - <strong>Uncle Shi</strong> in the lead, carrying a heavy sixty-pound horn-bow slung across his back, a quiver of twelve broadhead iron arrows, and his thirty-pound ash-shafted boar-spear; - <strong>Lu Chen</strong> walking on the right flank, wearing a thick sheepskin coat belted tightly over his patched hemp tunic, Widow Qin's hyena-hide bracers on his forearms, a forty-jin pack of salt, rope, and iron skinning-hooks on his back, and his <strong>eighty-jin solid Cold-Slag Boar-Spear</strong> carried effortlessly at a low trail across his palm; - Veteran hunters <strong>Old Ma</strong>, <strong>Big-Tall Zhao Da</strong>, and <strong>Cross-Eyed Liu</strong> in the center with short axes, hemp nets, and thirty-pound spears; - And fourteen-year-old <strong>Shi Tou</strong> bringing up the rear of the center column, carrying the medicine pouch of Granny Liu's ash-root powder, spare coils of rawhide rope, and a Newly forged twenty-pound iron-tipped short spear that Lu Chen had made for him out of mattock trimmings.</p>
<p>At the palisade gate, <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong> stood with his splinted right arm wrapped in his sheepskin cloak, beside <strong>Widow Qin</strong> and little blind <strong>Xiao Ya</strong>.</p>
<p>"Don't get greedy in the inner woods, Uncle Shi, Lu Chen," Old Tie Shan called out softly so his voice wouldn't carry up the ridge. "Four hundred jin of boar or wolf meat is enough to see the village through to the twelfth month. If you cross tracks with <strong>One-Eyed Wolf Han Wu's</strong> poachers, leave the carcass and pull back to the shale cliffs—no dead beast is worth trading lives with a <strong>Peak Tier Three</strong> cutthroat!"</p>
<p>"Keep the hearth warm, Old Tie," Lu Chen nodded once, his left thumb brushing the rusted three-inch iron collar on his spear shaft. "We'll be back before the fifth sunset."</p>
<p>For the first three miles above Frost-Tooth Gully, the trail climbed through stunted, wind-twisted scrub pines coated in greasy black frost. Not a single bird call or rodent rustle broke the dead silence; the only sounds were the crunch of six pairs of rawhide boots breaking through the frozen crust and the rhythmic, misty puffs of their breath under the tenfold gravity.</p>
<p>By mid-morning, they reached the <strong>Dead-Pine Boundary</strong>—a jagged ridge spine where three lightning-blasted iron-pine trunks stood like charred gallows against the grey sky.</p>
<p>Beyond that ridge, the terrain dropped sharply into the <strong>Inner Black-Briar Basin</strong>: a sprawling, five-mile labyrinth of dark, mist-choked ravines where subterranean sulfur vents kept the black-briar thickets from freezing completely, creating a steaming, thorny jungle of mud-bogs, razor-sharp brambles, and rotting deadfalls.</p>
<p>As soon as they descended fifty yards into the thick yellow-grey sulfur mist of the inner basin, Lu Chen felt the ambient <strong>Desolate Ash-Qi</strong> thicken to twice the density of the village below.</p>
<p>Even more noticeably, within the three-pace sphere of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>, the black-briar vines coiling across the ravine floor were no ordinary plants—their two-inch thorns were metallic grey, hard enough to pierce three layers of boiled leather, and the mud beneath the fallen pine needles was warm and treacherous.</p>
<p>"Step only on the exposed granite roots or where my heel leaves a clean print," Uncle Shi whispered, holding up his three-fingered left hand to halt the column beside a steaming sulfur puddle. Pointing with his spear-tip toward a patch of churned black mud and snapped two-inch briar vines at the edge of the thicket, the old hunter's face turned grave. "Fresh wallow—less than two hours old. Look at the depth of the cloven hoof-prints in the clay, and the height where the mud is rubbed against that iron-pine bark."</p>
<p>Lu Chen squatted beside the pine trunk and measured the deeply gouged tusk-scrapes four feet up the bark with his fingers.</p>
<p>"At least six hundred pounds on the hoof—a mature <strong>Early Tier Two Iron-Bristle Boar</strong>," Lu Chen murmured quietly. "And look at the edge of the mud wallow, Uncle Shi... it didn't leave at a walk. Something spooked it heading east toward the <strong>Bog-Viper Creek</strong>."</p>
