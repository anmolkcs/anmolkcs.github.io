<p>For the next two days—the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth of the eleventh month—Ash-Brook Village made its final preparations.</p>
<p>First, Uncle Shi sent two fast youths up the high ridge goat-path with two hundred jin of salted boar meat and fifty jin of acorn meal to keep the forty women, children, and elderly survivors of <strong>Mud-Well Village</strong> alive in their rock caves until the pass was cleared. In return, <strong>Hunter Feng</strong> (despite his bandaged left arm) and two surviving <strong>Tier-One</strong> Mud-Well hunters insisted on joining Ash-Brook's twelve-man assault column to avenge their slain brothers.</p>
<p>Second, inside the smithy shed, Lu Chen spent two final nights suspended in the <strong>Four-Cornered Iron-Pine Bow-Rack</strong>, pushing the traction weight up to <strong>three thousand five hundred jin</strong> by hanging two extra slag-iron blocks from the bent pine tops.</p>
<p>By the morning of the thirtieth, <strong>Thirty-Three of his Thirty-Six Major Sinew Lines</strong>—every tendon from his heels, knees, hips, spine, and shoulders down to his elbows and wrists—had lengthened and turned translucent gold-white! Only the last three deep digital flexor-tendons inside his palms remained half-drawn, waiting for the ultimate life-or-death shock to snap all thirty-six lines into <strong>Complete Tier Three (Bowstring-Sinew Drawing)</strong> unison!</p>
<p>Even at thirty-three drawn sinews, when Lu Chen flexed his back and snapped his eighty-jin solid black-iron boar-spear forward in the shed, the elastic recoil of his spine and shoulders generated <strong>three thousand 3,400 jin</strong> of explosive striking force—producing a sharp, dry <em>CRACK</em> like a bullwhip breaking the sound barrier!</p>
<p>Before dawn on the first day of the twelfth month, the <strong>Great Solstice Blizzard</strong> descended on the Black-Briar Barren Ridge.</p>
<p>Driven by sixty-mile-an-hour arctic gales from the northern badlands, horizontal sheets of blinding grey-white snow and ice-pellets turned the world outside the palisade into a howling abyss.</p>
<p>At the southern gate, the <strong>Two Iron-Clad Tribute Sledges</strong> stood hitched and ready—loaded with the <strong>eight hundred and twelve jin of Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron</strong>, thirty-four taels of silver, four <strong>Triple-Prod Crossbows</strong>, twelve iron-boss bucklers, and three days of frozen meat rations.</p>
<p>Twelve men stood in the torchlight wrapped in thick boar- and wolf-skin coats over leather brigandines: - <strong>Lu Chen</strong> at the point, carrying his eighty-jin solid Cold-Slag Boar-Spear across his shoulder and Scar-Six's fifty-pound broad-axe at his belt; - <strong>Uncle Shi</strong> (Early Tier Two) and <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong>—who had strapped a thirty-pound short-handled smith's war-hammer to his right wrist and flatly refused to stay behind while his son's freedom was being fought for; - <strong>Big-Tall Zhao</strong>, <strong>Cross-Eyed Liu</strong>, <strong>Old Ma</strong>, <strong>Hunter Feng</strong> and his two Mud-Well men, two stout village shield-men, and fourteen-year-old <strong>Shi Tou</strong>, who wore a double-layered hyena-hide vest and carried the spare crossbow quivers behind the armored sledge-boards.</p>
<p>Just before the gate bar was lifted, <strong>Widow Qin</strong> and little blind <strong>Xiao Ya</strong> hurried through the swirling snow to the head of the column.</p>
<p>Her small fingers red with cold, seven-year-old Xiao Ya held up two braided cords of dyed <strong>Red Hemp and Dried Blood-Moss</strong>—woven by her and her mother over the last two nights—and tied one around fourteen-year-old Shi Tou's wrist, and reached up to tie the second around Lu Chen's right wrist above his hyena-hide bracer.</p>
<p>"Granny Liu says the red moss-cord keeps the snow-ghosts from losing your footprints in the pass," little Xiao Ya said, her milky grey eyes lifted bravely into the howling snow toward Lu Chen's face. "Big Brother Lu—bring Uncle Tie's big brother Tie Niu back, and don't let the cold wind blow out your fire."</p>
<p>Lu Chen knelt on one knee in the snow so his eyes were level with the little blind girl's face, tightening the red hemp cord securely around his wrist.</p>
<p>"Wait by the hearth, Xiao Ya," Lu Chen said softly. "When we come back up this road, we're bringing the <strong>Clear-Eye Frost-Grass</strong> from Black-Rock Town so you can see the spring lanterns."</p>
