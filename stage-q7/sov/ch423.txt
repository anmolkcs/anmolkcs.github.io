<p>Eight hundred jin of nine-folded Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron meant eighty finished ten-jin bars—requiring over four thousand jin of raw Black-Marrow Ore to be pried out of the freezing red shale of Frost-Tooth Gully, carried down two miles of icy switchbacks under twelvefold gravity, smelted in thirty-bushel heaps of pine charcoal, and struck more than <strong>seventy thousand times</strong> with the forty-pound sledge.</p>
<p>To an outsider from Black-Rock Town, it was an impossible labor for a village whose master blacksmith had a broken collarbone.</p>
<p>Yet Ash-Brook Village did not let Lu Chen swing that hammer alone.</p>
<p>Once the seventy-three villagers realized that the quiet young stranger in Old Tie's shed was working sixteen hours a day to keep <strong>Tie Niu</strong> from being buried alive in Shaft #7—and to keep Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan from seizing the village anvil—every household that had ever had a hoe sharpened or a cauldron patched by Old Tie Shan came out into the snow.</p>
<p>In the mornings, while Lu Chen hammered the first four heats at the anvil, <strong>Uncle Shi</strong> led every able-bodied man and older boy up Frost-Tooth Gully with iron pry-bars, following the soapstone chalk marks Lu Chen had drawn along the horizontal frost-seams of the cliff so they could pry out the oily blue-black core nodules without triggering rockfalls.</p>
<p>In the afternoons, the village grandmothers and young children sat in a circle around the outer sorting-stones, their frost-reddened fingers wrapped in rags as they chipped away the barren red shale with cobbles so only pure ore went into the furnace.</p>
<p>And three times every day—at dawn, high noon, and midnight—<strong>Widow Qin</strong> and little blind <strong>Xiao Ya</strong> walked through the snow to the smithy shed carrying a covered clay pot of thick <strong>Iron-Husk Millet Gruel</strong> boiled with strips of dried hyena meat and wild blood-moss contributed pinch by pinch from forty different family jars.</p>
<p>On the sixth night of the marathon forging—the twenty-first of the tenth month—a howling blizzard slammed down from the upper peaks of the Black-Briar Ridge, burying the village lanes knee-deep in grey ash-snow.</p>
<p>Inside the smithy shed, it was past midnight. The four village boys had long since been sent home to sleep by Old Tie Shan, who had tied a leather loop around his waist so he could lean his own torso backward to pull the bellows lever while Lu Chen finished the nineteenth ten-jin bar of Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron.</p>
<p>Lu Chen's bare back and shoulders were covered in a sixth and seventh layer of charred, cracking black herb-crust. After more than fifteen thousand swings of the forty-pound sledge over six days, the muscle fibers beneath his skin were swollen tight as iron cables, and every pore across his torso, arms, and legs was clogged with a dense, baked glaze of sweat, pulverized iron-scale, and <strong>Blood-Briar Sap</strong>.</p>
<p>He felt as though he were encased inside a sealed iron coffin that was slowly shrinking around his heart. His skin could no longer sweat freely; the internal furnace heat built up inside his torso until his breath came out in scorching white jets that smelled of hot rust, and a dull roaring filled his ears.</p>
<p>"Lu Chen—stop after this fold!" Old Tie Shan wheezed from the bellows strap, seeing the terrifying crimson flush beneath the black crust on the young man's neck. "Your pores are sealed shut by the seventh ash-glaze! When a man tries to push from Early Tier One to <strong>Complete Ash-Skin Scouring</strong> in a single fortnight, if the seventh husk doesn't split before the heat hits his heart, he'll boil his own brain in his skull! Go jump in the snowdrift outside to cool down!"</p>
<p>Through the roaring heat in his temples, Lu Chen kept his consciousness anchored in the three-pace tranquility of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>.</p>
<p>He could feel every drop of blood pounding through his carotid arteries at twice its normal pressure; he could also feel the microscopic network of taut, calcified black skin-husk covering his entire body from ankle to neck—waiting for just one more surge of internal pressure to shatter from the inside out.</p>
<p>If he jumped into the snowdrift now to cool the heat, the seventh husk would settle without shedding, and it would take another month of slow grinding to reach this threshold again. They did not have another month—Tie Niu had fifty-nine days left before Pit #7 flooded.</p>
<p>"One more heat, Old Tie!" Lu Chen's voice rang out like struck bronze. "Give me full blast on the twentieth bar!"</p>
