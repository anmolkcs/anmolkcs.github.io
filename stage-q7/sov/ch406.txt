<p>When Widow Qin and little Xiao Ya departed down the mud lane, Lu Chen stripped off the tattered rags of his old tunic, pulled on the thick, coarsely stitched hemp work-shirt—which smelled cleanly of wood-ash soap and dried pine needles—and walked over to the massive <strong>Ox-Hide Double-Chamber Bellows</strong> behind the stone hearth.</p>
<p>In a mortal smithy of the lower realms, pulling a bellows was light apprentice work. In Ash-Brook Village, it was an ordeal that broke weak men's backs.</p>
<p>Because the local <strong>Black-Slag Bog Ore</strong> was infused with stubborn Origin-Grain, an ordinary charcoal fire could not soften it; the hearth had to be fed with dense <strong>Bitter-Pine Charcoal</strong> and blasted with a continuous, high-pressure gale of air until the core of the furnace reached a blinding white-yellow heat. To generate that pressure under tenfold gravity, the village bellows was constructed from three layers of cured ridge-ox hide reinforced with iron ribs, operated by a six-foot ash-wood lever weighted with a hundred-and-twenty-pound counter-stone.</p>
<p>"Don't yank it with your shoulders," Old Tie Shan instructed, tossing two shovelfuls of crushed black-slag ore into the glowing bed of charcoal. "My old striker boy tried to muscle the lever with his arms on his first week and tore his rotator tendon out of the socket. Plant your heels wide in the cinder-grooves. Sink your hips as the lever comes down, let your ribs hang like an iron bucket, and push through the earth with your legs on the return stroke. And whatever you do, don't breathe through your mouth when the yellow sulfur-smoke puffs back from the tuyere pipe, or you'll be coughing blood by noon."</p>
<p>Lu Chen placed both bruised hands on the worn ash-wood handle. The wood was polished slick by years of sweat and dried blood—some of it belonging to Old Tie's dead striker, and before that, to Old Tie's son Tie Niu.</p>
<p>He sank his mind into <strong>Prism Stillness</strong> and drew the lever down.</p>
<p><em>WHOOOOOOOSH—!</em></p>
<p>The heavy ox-hide chamber compressed against the stiff air resistance of the tuyere nozzle, sending a roaring jet of oxygen into the heart of the charcoal bed. Simultaneously, the recoil of the hundred-and-twenty-pound counter-stone pulled upward against Lu Chen's palms, sending a dull lance of pain through his three cracked left ribs and across the raw blisters on his fingers.</p>
<p><em>Stroke one.</em></p>
<p>Lu Chen did not use any nonexistent True Essence. Instead, he applied the purest, most fundamental biomechanical principle he had learned when practicing ten thousand basic spear thrusts on Earth and in Cloudstride City: <strong>Six-Harmony Structural Alignment</strong>—ankle, knee, hip, spine, shoulder, and wrist locked into a single truss so that no single bruised muscle bore the load alone.</p>
<p>As the lever rose on the intake stroke, he inhaled a slow, shallow breath through his nose, using the tiny nasal hairs and a thin film of moisture in his throat to trap the flying coal-ash. As the lever descended on the blast stroke, he exhaled steadily through pursed lips, tightening his abdominal wall around his cracked left ribs like an internal splint.</p>
<p><em>Stroke ten.</em> <em>Stroke fifty.</em> <em>Stroke two hundred.</em></p>
<p>Within half an hour, the temperature inside the smithy shed climbed from freezing sleet to a blistering oven-roast on his face, while the wind at his back remained icy cold. The newly healed scabs on his palms split open against the rough ash-wood handle, smearing sticky crimson across the wood. His starved leg muscles quivered as lactic acid and invasive Desolate Ash-Qi built up in his calves, threatening to lock his knees into violent cramps.</p>
<p>Every time a muscle fiber began to spasm, Lu Chen used the three-pace clarity of <em>Prism Stillness</em> to shift his foot angle by a tenth of an inch on the cinder floor, transferring the load from his outer thigh to his inner hamstring while keeping the rhythm of the air-blast completely unchanged.</p>
<p>At the anvil, Old Tie Shan pulled a glowing orange bar of pig-iron from the fire with his tongs, clamping the tongs to his waist with a leather strap so his single right hand could wield the twelve-pound rounding hammer.</p>
<p><em>CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!</em></p>
<p>For two hours, the old man did not look back once at the bellows. Yet as he worked through the third heat of iron, Old Tie Shan's brow furrowed in quiet astonishment.</p>
<p>Usually, even a seasoned village youth grew erratic after three hundred strokes—jerking the lever too hard when tired, or letting the air pressure drop between breaths so the furnace core cooled from white-yellow to dull cherry. Yet behind him, the <em>whoosh—pause—whoosh</em> of the ox-hide bellows was as steady, unhurried, and relentless as an underground water-clock. Not once in seven hundred strokes had the furnace flame dipped by a single shade of color.</p>
<p>When Old Tie Shan finally quenched the finished mattock-blade in the brine barrel with a loud <em>HSSSSSS</em> and turned around to call for a rest, he stopped short.</p>
<p>Lu Chen stood at the bellows lever, his hemp shirt soaked black with sweat, blood dripping slowly from the heel of his left palm onto the cinders, his chest rising and falling in deep, silent waves—yet his dark eyes were clear and tranquil as a winter well, waiting for the next shovel of ore.</p>
