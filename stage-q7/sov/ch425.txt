<p>After completing the seventh skin-shedding and stepping firmly into <strong>Complete Tier One of the Mortal-Shedding Stage</strong>, Lu Chen finally slept for six straight hours on the straw pallet in the lean-to.</p>
<p>When he woke at dawn on the twenty-second day of the tenth month, the nagging ache that had lingered in his left ribs and lungs since his fall through the dimensional trench was completely gone. Under the tenfold Desolate Gravity, his body no longer felt as if it were dragging a waterlogged pine log; when he rolled off the straw and stood upright, his bare feet gripped the packed earth with quiet, springy power, and the freezing draft seeping through the mud wall felt no colder than an autumn breeze in Warm-Spring Valley.</p>
<p>He reached up to his chest and untied the hemp cord holding the <strong>three-inch rusted iron collar</strong> of his shattered Sovereign Spear.</p>
<p>For twenty-one days, he had worn that pitted scrap of metal against his skin because he had neither the strength nor the iron to forge a weapon worthy of holding it.</p>
<p>Now, with twenty of the eighty bars of Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron already stacked on the rack and the village ore-hauling crew running like clockwork, it was time to forge his own fangs.</p>
<p>Stepping out into the smithy shed before the village boys arrived for the morning bellows shift, Lu Chen dragged a heavy wooden crate out from beneath the cooling bench.</p>
<p>Inside the crate lay nearly a hundred and ten jin of <strong>trimmed heel-offcuts</strong>—the dense, two-inch triangular end-pieces that Lu Chen had chiselled off the bottom of each <strong>Black-Marrow Core</strong> billet after expelling the spiral slag-veins over the past week. Because those heel-pieces sat right next to the slag-bleed channel, an ordinary blacksmith discarded them as scrap; yet with the three-pace microscopic clarity of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>, Lu Chen had carefully trimmed away the brittle outer slag rinds each night, saving only the purest, most heavily compressed nuggets of high-density dark-blue core iron.</p>
<p>"You've been hoarding those heel-nuggets under the bench for seven nights," Old Tie Shan's voice came from the doorway of the lean-to as the old man stepped out, his broken collarbone still bound in splints, though the color had returned to his cheeks. "What are you planning to forge with a hundred jin of core-trim, Lu Chen? A woodsman's axe?"</p>
<p>"Not an axe, Old Tie," Lu Chen said, dumping the hundred and ten jin of dark-blue iron nuggets into a clay crucible beside the hearth. "The eleventh month starts in eight days. Uncle Shi told me yesterday that once the deep snow closes the upper ridges, the village hunting party has to go into the inner <strong>Black-Briar Woods</strong> to hunt <strong>Iron-Bristle Boars</strong> and <strong>Ridge Wolves</strong>—both to feed the seventy mouths in Ash-Brook through the winter famine, and to gather beast-galls and pelts for the remaining silver we need at the tally-house."</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan frowned. "The inner Black-Briar Woods are full of <strong>Peak Tier One</strong> and <strong>Tier Two</strong> beasts—and worse, the <strong>Broken-Tooth Poachers</strong> led by that one-eyed deserter <strong>Han Wu</strong> prowl the inner creeks every winter. Uncle Shi's thirty-pound hunting spear bounced right off a big boar's shoulder plate last winter. With your <strong>Complete Tier One</strong> strength of twelve hundred jin, an ordinary village spear will snap in your hands the first time you thrust full-force."</p>
<p>"That's why I'm not forging a thirty-pound spear," Lu Chen said quietly, laying the rusted three-inch iron collar of his former Sovereign Spear on the edge of the anvil block. "I'm forging an <strong>eighty-jin solid-shaft heavy boar-spear</strong>—from butt-spike to spearhead, one continuous rod of nine-folded Cold-Slag Core Iron."</p>
