<p>Three hundred yards above the granite overhang, Frost-Tooth Gully narrowed into a jagged throat of blood-red shale where an ancient landslide had exposed a four-foot seam of <strong>Black-Slag Bog Iron</strong>.</p>
<p>Here the wind howled through natural flutes in the rock with a sound like weeping women. On the stunted, thorny branches of a dead iron-pine jutting from the cliff above sat four <strong>Iron-Beak Ridge Crows</strong>—carrion birds the size of geese, with greasy black feathers that shed sleet like oiled slate and curved gray beaks strong enough to crack a sheep's skull. Their yellow eyes fixed unblinkingly on the two humans climbing into the quarry pit below.</p>
<p>"Don't mind those bastards unless there's more than a dozen of them," Shi Tou muttered, spitting into his palms and gripping his short pickaxe. "They won't dive on a live man who's still swinging iron, though they'll peck your eyes out quick enough if a falling shale slab pins your leg. Watch the upper lip of the seam, Big Brother Lu—when you pry out the black nodules, the rotten red rock above likes to calve off in three-hundred-pound sheets."</p>
<p>Lu Chen nodded and stepped up to the exposed rock face.</p>
<p>In his past life at the peak of the Star-Soul Universe, a wave of his sleeve could have pulverized this entire mountain range into cosmic dust. Now, standing in the freezing mud under twelvefold gravity with a six-foot, thirty-pound iron pry-bar in his bandaged hands, he was just a mortal laborer facing a wall of stubborn stone.</p>
<p>Yet he did not feel humiliated by the task. Instead, as he laid his palm against the freezing red shale and let <strong>Prism Stillness</strong> settle over the rock face within three paces, a quiet, grounded focus filled his chest.</p>
<p>Every rock formation had a grain, just as every ancient bronze vessel and every martial posture had cleavage planes. To the naked eye, the red shale wall was a chaotic jumble of frozen stone embedding heavy black iron nodules. To Lu Chen's restorer's eye, sharpened by <em>Prism Stillness</em>, a network of microscopic frost-wedged hairlines ran horizontally beneath three prime clusters of high-density black ore.</p>
<p>Instead of swinging a pickaxe blindly for twenty minutes and jarring his newly knitted ribs with every blow, Lu Chen wedged the chisel-point of his thirty-pound pry-bar a quarter-inch into a barely visible horizontal seam beneath the largest ore cluster.</p>
<p>He stepped back half a pace, planted his rawhide sandals in the figura-eight infantry stance, inhaled a slow breath of freezing air into his lower abdomen, and threw his whole body weight downward onto the end of the lever in one explosive, unified pulse.</p>
<p><em>CRACK—RUMBLE!</em></p>
<p>With a sharp report like snapping pine timber, the frost-seam split three feet across. Five dense, fist-sized nodules of top-grade <strong>Black-Marrow Bog Ore</strong>—each glinting with a dull, oily metallic blue rather than the porous gray of surface slag—tumbled cleanly out of the cliff into the gravel at their feet, without disturbing the unstable overhang of rotten shale above!</p>
<p>Shi Tou, who had been mid-swing with his pickaxe six feet away, froze with his mouth open. He scrambled over, picked up one of the oily blue-black nodules with both hands, and gasped at its weight.</p>
<p>"Black-Marrow Core!" Shi Tou whispered, his voice cracking with excitement. "Grandpa and Uncle Tie only find two or three nodules this pure in a whole month! One lump of this smelts down into twice as much clean iron as surface slag, and it barely has any cold-shatter ash in it! How did you know it was hiding behind that flat shelf?"</p>
<p>"Listen to the sound when you tap the rock," Lu Chen said, tapping the cliff face twice with the tip of his pry-bar—once on hollow rotten shale (<em>thud</em>), and once over a dense iron pocket (<em>clink</em>). "Hollow shale sounds like wet mud; solid ore-heart rings against the bar. Stand back by the boulder and sort the clean nodules into the baskets while I pry out the lower seam."</p>
<p>For the next hour and a half, under the watchful yellow eyes of the Ridge Crows and the worsening sleet, Lu Chen worked the thirty-pound pry-bar along the cliff face with methodical precision.</p>
<p>It was grueling, unglamorous work. Even with perfect leverage, driving the iron bar into frozen stone under twelvefold gravity made the bandaged wounds on his palms bleed fresh crimson through the linen, and the bitter cold bit through his thin hemp trousers until his shins burned like ice. Yet with every heave of the bar, his starved back muscles, latissimus dorsi, and forearms were stretched and compressed under heavy load, squeezing out stagnant fluid and forcing the hot calories of his morning millet gruel into the torn muscle fibers.</p>
<p>By the time the midday sky darkened with a coming snow-squall, Shi Tou's basket was loaded with eighty jin of oily blue-black core nodules, and Lu Chen's basket held sixty jin—enough high-grade ore to finish the entire autumn tribute with thirty jin to spare.</p>
<p>As they shouldered the creaking rattan straps and started down the slick shale switchbacks, one of the four Iron-Beak Ridge Crows suddenly launched off the dead pine branch, riding a downdraft straight toward the back of Shi Tou's neck as the boy slipped half an inch on wet gravel!</p>
<p>Lu Chen didn't shout. Pivoting on his right heel despite the sixty-jin basket crushing his spine, he whipped the thirty-pound iron pry-bar upward in a short, compact <strong>Blocking Thrust</strong>—the most basic move of northern spearsmanship.</p>
<p><em>WHACK!</em></p>
<p>The blunt iron tip caught the diving ten-pound carrion bird squarely beneath the breastbone in midair, snapping its keel-bone with a wet crunch and sending the carcass tumbling into the gully snow.</p>
<p>Lu Chen didn't even pause his stride; he hooked the dead crow by one scaly claw with the tip of his pry-bar, tossed it atop his ore basket, and steadied Shi Tou's shoulder with his free hand.</p>
<p>"Keep your eyes on the trail, Little Stone," Lu Chen said calmly as the remaining three crows fled croaking into the mist. "Crow meat is tough and gamey, but boiled with ginger-root, the broth will keep your grandfather's joints warm tonight."</p>
