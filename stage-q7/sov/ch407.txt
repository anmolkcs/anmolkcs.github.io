<p>"Let go of the handle, boy. Are you trying to weld your skin to the ash-wood?"</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan's gruff voice broke the rhythmic roar of the hearth. Stepping over to the water-tub with a dipper of cold salt-brine and a rag smeared with mutton tallow, the one-armed smith grabbed Lu Chen's wrist and pried his stiffened fingers off the bellows lever one by one.</p>
<p>The skin across the base of Lu Chen's four fingers and the meat of his thumb had blistered, burst, and rubbed raw against the wood grain, leaving four crescent-shaped patches of exposed red dermis stinging in the coal smoke.</p>
<p>Without asking permission, Old Tie Shan poured a splash of stinging salt-brine over Lu Chen's palms to wash out the coal dust, then rubbed a thick layer of yellow mutton tallow mixed with powdered <strong>Char-Willow Bark</strong> into the raw flesh before wrapping both hands tightly in strips of boiled linen.</p>
<p>"In this world, soft hands mean a short life," Old Tie Shan muttered, tying off the linen knot with his teeth and his right thumb. "Before a man can enter the <strong>First Tier of the Mortal-Shedding Stage—Ash-Skin Scouring</strong>—his skin has to tear, bleed, and scab over a hundred times against iron, heat, and ash-root. Don't baby those blisters, and don't let them fester either. Come look at the anvil."</p>
<p>Lu Chen flexed his bandaged fingers, feeling the cool willow-bark tallow dull the throbbing burn, and walked to the side of the stone anvil-block.</p>
<p>Laid out across a flat slate cooling-table were six rough, brick-sized ingots of smelted <strong>Black-Slag Bog Iron</strong> that the village laborers had dug from the lower creek beds, alongside three finished iron mattock-heads meant for the upcoming autumn tribute to Black-Rock Frontier Town. One of the finished mattock-heads had snapped cleanly in half near the eye-socket when Old Tie tested it against a granite block that morning.</p>
<p>"Look at that break," Old Tie Shan said bitterly, tapping the snapped mattock-head with his tongs. "The <strong>Black-Briar Ridge</strong> ore is cursed with what we smiths call <strong>Cold-Shatter Veins</strong>—threads of brittle gray phosphorus-ash trapped inside the iron grain by the Desolate Ash-Qi. From the outside, the ingot looks solid. You heat it, hammer it for half a day, quench it, and one strike against hard rock snaps it like a dry radish. With only one arm left, I can't swing the forty-pound heavy sledge fast enough while the iron is at peak white heat to drive the cold-shatter slag out of the center. If Sub-Bailiff <strong>Zhao Kuan</strong> breaks even two of our thirty tribute mattocks during inspection on the fifteenth, he'll fine the village another five hundred jin of refined iron—or take another boy to Pit #7."</p>
<p>Lu Chen stepped closer to the cooling-table, bringing the broken mattock-head and the six unworked ingots within the three-pace sphere of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>.</p>
<p>On Modern Earth, before his transmigration to the Sky-Spill Continent, Lu Chen had spent years in a provincial museum conservation lab restoring corroded Warring States iron agricultural tools and Han Dynasty ring-pommel sabers. Even without spiritual sense, his eyes knew how to read crystalline fracture surfaces, slag inclusions, and thermal stress lines. And with <em>Prism Stillness</em> sharpening his bare mortal eyesight to microscopic clarity within three paces, the exposed break of the mattock-head was like an open book.</p>
<p>He leaned down until his nose was six inches from the jagged gray metal, studying how the tiny dull-white flecks of ash-slag clustered along diagonal shear planes inside the iron. Then he picked up one of the six unworked five-pound ingots—which weighed fifty pounds in his hands under the frontier's tenfold gravity—and turned it slowly toward the orange light of the forge.</p>
<p>Across the rough, pitted surface of the raw ingot ran tiny, hair-thin dimples of bluish-gray oxide that were almost invisible to Old Tie Shan's cataract-clouded eye.</p>
<p>"Old Tie," Lu Chen said quietly, tracing a fingernail along a curved line on the side of the raw ingot. "The cold-shatter ash doesn't run straight down the middle of the ingot. Because the bog-ore was washed down the creek under high gravity, the ash-slag settles in spiral layers, like the rings of a twisted pine tree."</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan frowned, leaning in close. "Spiral layers?"</p>
<p>"Look here at this bluish dimple on the heel of the ingot, and where it comes out two inches higher on the opposite face," Lu Chen explained, picking up a piece of white soapstone chalk from the anvil ledge and drawing three angled diagonal lines across the rough iron block. "If we lay the ingot flat on the anvil and hammer straight down on its broad face, we fold the ash-ring inward and trap it right where the mattock eye-socket gets punched. That's why it snapped. But if we heat the heel first, stand the ingot on its corner at a thirty-degree tilt, and strike along these three chalk lines from the bottom up, the slag-vein will squeeze out through the end like pus from a boil before we ever flatten the blade."</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan stared at the three soapstone lines on the ingot, then grabbed the broken mattock-head and squinted at the spiral pattern of the gray fracture face. For ten full seconds, the only sound in the shed was the crackle of pine charcoal.</p>
<p>Slowly, the old blacksmith's single hand tightened on his tongs.</p>
<p>"My grandfather forged iron in Black-Rock Town for forty years," Old Tie Shan whispered hoarsely, looking up at Lu Chen as if seeing him for the first time, "and he never knew the bog-slag ran in spirals. Where did you learn to read iron like that, boy?"</p>
<p>"Back home," Lu Chen said softly, looking down at the rusted three-inch spear collar tied over his chest, "I used to mend broken old things so people wouldn't forget who made them."</p>
