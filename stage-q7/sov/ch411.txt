<p>Before dawn on the seventh morning, the blackened crust of Granny Liu's caustic paste had dried into a tight, burning shell across Lu Chen's palms, forearms, and shoulders. Every movement of his joints made the dried crust crackle like thin ice, pulling painfully at the raw new skin growing underneath.</p>
<p>Yet when Old Tie Shan raked open the banked coals of the hearth and laid three fist-sized nodules of blue-black <strong>Black-Marrow Core Ore</strong> into the center of the fire, Lu Chen did not walk to the bellows lever. Instead, he stepped to the corner of the shed, wrapped both stinging, crusted hands around the four-foot ash-wood shaft of the <strong>forty-pound iron striker's sledge</strong>, and lifted it from the floor.</p>
<p>Under the tenfold Desolate Gravity of Ash-Brook Village, a forty-pound sledgehammer carried the downward deadweight of <strong>four hundred jin</strong> in the lower worlds—and once swung in an overhead arc, its centrifugal momentum exceeded eight hundred jin. For an uncultivated mortal who had not yet even stepped into the First Tier of the Mortal-Shedding Stage, swinging such a hammer cleanly without letting the rebound wrench his spine or shatter his wrists was a test of life and death.</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan gave the bellows twenty quick strokes with his single arm until the blue-black ore nodules fused into a single, sputtering ball of white-hot <strong>bloom-iron</strong>, then clamped his long tongs around the glowing mass and swung it onto the center of the flat stone-and-iron anvil.</p>
<p>"Listen carefully, Lu Chen!" Old Tie Shan shouted over the roar of the fire, holding his twelve-pound guide-hammer in his right hand while clamping the long tongs between his left thigh and leather apron. "In striker-work, my small hammer is the eye and the tongue! Wherever I tap the bloom once, you bring the big sledge down on that exact spot! If I tap twice on the side of the anvil, you stop mid-swing! And remember—Black-Marrow iron cools twice as fast under the ridge wind! We only get eighteen strikes per heat before the grain stiffens! If your sledge lands half an inch crooked, the white-hot slag will spray straight into our faces!"</p>
<p>Lu Chen planted his rawhide sandals shoulder-width apart on the packed cinders.</p>
<p>He did not lift the heavy sledge with brute arm strength. Instead, drawing upon <strong>Prism Stillness</strong> and the structural body-mechanics of the <strong>Heavy Spear</strong>, he treated the four-foot sledgehammer shaft as a short, front-weighted spear.</p>
<p><em>TINK!</em></p>
<p>Old Tie Shan's guide-hammer tapped the high center of the glowing white iron bloom, right along the first spiral slag-line Lu Chen had mapped two days earlier.</p>
<p>Lu Chen pivoted his back hip, let the forty-pound iron head swing up along a tight vertical circle close to his centerline so the lever-arm never pulled his healing left ribs out of alignment, and dropped his entire bodyweight through his knees as the sledge fell!</p>
<p><em>BOOM—CLANG!</em></p>
<p>The forty-pound flat face struck the exact millimeter Old Tie's guide-hammer had marked. A shower of blinding white-yellow sparks and liquid phosphorus-slag burst horizontally out of the bottom seam of the bloom—away from both men's boots—as the spongy iron compressed by half an inch under the tremendous impact!</p>
<p>The shockwave traveling back up the ash-wood handle was brutal. Through the dried black crust of Granny Liu's paste, the skin of Lu Chen's palms split along three old creases, and a numbing vibration rattled all the way up his radius bones to his teeth.</p>
<p>Yet just before the rebound could jar his elbows, Lu Chen relaxed his grip by a fraction of a second—using the ancient spearman's technique of <strong>Unloading Force into the Earth</strong>—channeling the upward recoil down through his locked heels into the cinder floor so the rebounding hammer floated naturally back up to shoulder height for the second stroke!</p>
<p><em>TINK!</em> <em>BOOM—CLANG!</em> <em>TINK!</em> <em>BOOM—CLANG!</em></p>
<p>Strike after strike rang out across the snowy dawn of Ash-Brook Village like a deep bronze war-drum.</p>
<p>By the tenth strike, sweat was pouring down Lu Chen's chest, dissolving the edges of the caustic black herb-crust and making every open fissure on his shoulders burn as if rubbed with salt. By the fifteenth strike, his vision narrowed to a three-foot circle of blinding orange iron, the silver flash of Old Tie's guide-hammer, and the rhythm of his own roaring blood.</p>
<p>On the eighteenth strike—just as the bloom cooled from bright orange to a dull cherry red—Old Tie Shan tapped the side of the anvil twice: <em>Tink-tink!</em></p>
<p>Lu Chen arrested the falling forty-pound sledge two inches above the iron, letting its momentum swing safely past his right boot without touching the cooling metal.</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan lifted the compacted, rectangular bar of pure, slag-free wrought iron with his tongs and stared at its smooth, square edges. Not one of the eighteen heavy sledge-blows had missed the mark by the width of a fingernail; three days' worth of rough bloom-forging had been accomplished in a single heat.</p>
<p>The old blacksmith looked up at Lu Chen, whose chest was heaving like the ox-hide bellows as he leaned on the upright shaft of the sledgehammer, and let out a low, raspy laugh.</p>
<p>"Seven more heats like that, boy, and Zhao Kuan won't find a single flaw if he bites the iron with his own teeth!"</p>
