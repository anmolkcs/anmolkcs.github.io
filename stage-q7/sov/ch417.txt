<p>All through the fourteenth day of the tenth autumn month, Ash-Brook Village worked under a gray, low-hanging sky that smelled of iron and snow.</p>
<p>At the western palisade, Uncle Shi and the village men piled frozen boulders and wove fresh bundles of thorny black-briar vines across the gap where the snowdrift had allowed the hyenas to leap the wall. In the center of the village lane, two wide wooden sledges were loaded with the autumn tax quota demanded by <strong>Black-Rock Frontier Town</strong>: - Twenty-eight newly forged <strong>Black-Marrow Iron Mattocks</strong>, each fitted by Lu Chen and Old Tie Shan with a four-foot handle of fire-hardened mountain ash; - Three hundred jin of raw smelted pig-iron bars; - Twelve cured hides of ridge-boar and iron-jaw hyena; - And forty jin of dried <strong>Ash-Root</strong> harvested by the village women and children at the cost of frostbitten fingers.</p>
<p>Despite the biting pain of the cauterized fang-burn on his left forearm, Lu Chen spent the entire day at the smithy workbench shaved ash-wood handles with a drawknife and driving iron wedges into the mattock eyes.</p>
<p>Because he had broken into <strong>Early Tier One (Ash-Skin Scouring)</strong> the night before, his physical recovery speed under the Great Desolate Universe's dense nutrition had taken a qualitative leap. That noon, Granny Liu boiled the crushed thigh-bones and spine of the alpha Iron-Jaw Ridge Hyena with a pinch of <strong>Red-Shale Salt</strong> and three stalks of <strong>Blood-Moss</strong>, producing a pot of thick, milky-red <strong>Beast-Marrow Broth</strong>.</p>
<p>When Lu Chen drank two large bowls of the scorching marrow broth, he could feel his toughened outer skin acting like a pressurized furnace jacket—keeping the dense, hot vitality of the Tier-One beast marrow from dissipating through his pores and forcing it straight into his bruised muscles and the seared wound on his forearm. By nightfall, a tough, dark-red scab had already formed under the linen bandage, and his baseline grip strength had climbed steadily past <strong>nine hundred and fifty jin</strong>.</p>
<p>Late that evening, as the village gates were barred with double oak beams and the watch-fires were lit along the palisade, Widow Qin came quietly to the smithy shed with little Xiao Ya.</p>
<p>Using scraps of the alpha hyena's thick, wind-proof neck-hide that Uncle Shi had cured with alum and wood-ash during the day, Widow Qin had stitched a pair of heavy, fleece-lined <strong>Hyena-Hide Bracers</strong> reinforced with strips of split horn. She knelt beside the anvil block, strapped the left bracer gently over Lu Chen's bandaged forearm so no stray bump could reopen the cauterized wound, and strapped the right bracer over his hammer-hand wrist.</p>
<p>Little Xiao Ya reached out until her fingers brushed the rough hyena fur of the bracer on Lu Chen's arm.</p>
<p>"Big Brother Lu," the little blind girl whispered earnestly, "when the beast scratched the door last night, I wasn't scared once I heard your iron bar ring on the stone. When my eyes get better in the spring, I'm going to gather a whole basket of sweet pine-nuts for you."</p>
<p>Lu Chen rested his warm, calloused right hand lightly on the crown of the little girl's head. "It's a promise, Xiao Ya. When the spring snow melts on the ridge, we'll roast pine-nuts right here by Old Tie's hearth."</p>
<p>After they left, Old Tie Shan stood by the shed entrance looking up at the dark mountain pass to the south, where the road wound twenty-five miles down to Black-Rock Frontier Town.</p>
<p>"Tomorrow morning before noon, <strong>Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan</strong> and his riders will come through the gorge," Old Tie Shan said quietly, his single hand resting on the cold face of the anvil. "Lu Chen, when Zhao Kuan inspects the iron tomorrow, keep your head down and stand behind the bellows. You don't have your name on the Black-Rock County household register yet. If Zhao Kuan sees a young man with wide shoulders and <strong>Tier One Ash-Skin</strong> who isn't on the village rolls, he'll either demand ten taels of registration silver we don't have—or claim you're a runaway convict and clap you in iron chains for <strong>Pit #7</strong> on the spot."</p>
<p>Lu Chen looked out at the dark southern pass where the wind howled through the dead pines.</p>
<p>"I won't go looking for trouble, Old Tie," Lu Chen said calmly, banking the forge coals for the night. "Right now, my bones are still too light to break iron chains. I know when to bow my head—and I know how to remember a face."</p>
