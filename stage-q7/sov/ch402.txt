<p>Cold came before thought—a wet, suffocating, iron-scented cold that pressed against his ribs like a collapsed mine roof.</p>
<p>For a long time, Lu Chen could not tell whether he was still tumbling through the dimensional slag-trench or buried six feet beneath frozen soil. Every time he tried to draw air into his chest, a knife of rusted grit seemed to scrape from his windpipe down to the bottom of his lungs. In the dark behind his eyelids, fragments of memory flickered without order: the warm steam rising from his mother's bamboo steamer in Cloudstride City; Su Wan'er holding up a Newly forged inscription talisman with ink on her cheek; Su Qingxue's fingers reaching toward the crystal barrier as the gray time-frost sealed her eyes.</p>
<p><em>I can't die here,</em> a voice rasped in the back of his skull. <em>If I die in the mud, nobody in all the heavens knows where the cocoon is buried. Ten thousand years will pass, the seal will thin, and the bronze stakes will come back.</em></p>
<p>Lu Chen forced his crusted eyelids apart.</p>
<p>There were no star-rivers overhead, nor the fragrant cedar rafters of Warm-Spring Courtyard. Three feet above his face sagged a low roof of blackened thatch and mud-plastered slate, dripping soot-stained meltwater onto the straw beside his ear. He lay inside a cramped, windowless stone lean-to no wider than an ox-stall. The air reeked of wet coal-slag, rancid mutton tallow, and bitter mountain weeds.</p>
<p>Instinctively, he tried to push himself up on his right elbow.</p>
<p>His elbow did not budge. Instead, a dull tearing pain shot through his deltoid and collarbone, and his head fell back against the damp straw while black spots danced across his vision.</p>
<p>Lu Chen lay still for ten slow heartbeats, listening to the ragged whistle of his own lungs. His body—the body that had once carried a hundred-million-mile universe in every cell—was completely gone, burned away to fuel the Nine-Epoch Inverse Chrono-Cocoon. What remained was a battered, starved mortal frame stripped of every trace of True Essence, spiritual sea, and golden marrow. Worse still, an invisible, suffocating downward pressure lay upon every square inch of his skin, pinning his limbs to the earth as if he were wearing wet lead armor.</p>
<p><strong>Tenfold Desolate Gravity.</strong></p>
<p>Even with his cultivation crippled, two lifetimes of combat instinct allowed him to recognize the terrifying physical truth of this higher universe. Here in the <strong>Great Desolate Origin Universe</strong>, the soil, rock, water, and air were woven with dense <strong>Origin-Grain</strong>. A fist-sized cobble from a roadside ditch weighed as much as a block of forged iron in the lower realms; a gust of winter wind carried the momentum of swinging timber.</p>
<p>And the air itself was poison to an untempered body. As Lu Chen inhaled slowly through his nose, he felt thin, jagged filaments of grey <strong>Desolate Ash-Qi</strong> rasp down his throat. Unlike the gentle heaven-and-earth spirit energy of the Sky-Spill Continent, the ambient energy of this wasteland was raw, abrasive, and heavy with mineral frost. Without a tempered physical vessel to filter it, the Ash-Qi settled into mortal joints and lungs like fine stone-dust, turning flesh stiff and brittle before middle age.</p>
<p>Lu Chen closed his eyes and turned his focus inward toward his brow.</p>
<p>His dantian was a collapsed, dry scar. His twelve meridians were shriveled and blocked by clots of dead blood. And in the center of his mind, the <strong>Chrono-Soul Prism</strong> hung like a chipped, lusterless pebble of ordinary gray river-slate.</p>
<p>All twenty-seven Technique Memory Bubbles were gone. The inner space where the Nine-Petaled Ice Lotus had once floated was fused shut by a web of deep black fissures. It could no longer absorb beast blood-essence, purify pills, or project ancient scriptures.</p>
<p>Yet as Lu Chen steadied his jagged breathing against the tearing pain in his ribs, a faint, familiar coolness seeped from the cracked gray slate into his temples: <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>.</p>
<p>In this higher world of dense Origin-Grain, <em>Prism Stillness</em> could no longer scan a thousand miles or freeze an enemy's thoughts. It covered barely three paces around his body. All it gave him was an unshakable, ice-clear mental stillness that kept his mind from fracturing under physical torment, and a restorer's microscopic sensitivity to his own broken body—letting him feel which three ribs on his left side had hairline cracks, where the clotted blood was pooling in his lower lungs, and how much tension his torn shoulder tendon could bear before snapping.</p>
<p>Slowly, millimeter by millimeter, Lu Chen curled the stiff fingers of his left hand.</p>
<p>Pressed tightly against his split palm was a three-inch ring of pitted, rust-blackened iron—the broken collar-ferrule of his shattered Sovereign Spear. Every trace of divine law had been burned out of the metal, leaving it no different from scrap iron dug out of an old battlefield. Yet when the rough rust bit into his palm, the phantom ache in his chest quieted.</p>
<p>He was alive. And as long as his fingers could still close around iron, the road back to Warm-Spring Valley existed.</p>
