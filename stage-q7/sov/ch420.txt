<p>"Old Tie!"</p>
<p>Uncle Shi and fourteen-year-old Shi Tou lunged forward to catch the fallen blacksmith, while two of the mounted bailiff guards immediately cranked their heavy iron crossbows with a metallic <em>clack-clack</em>, leveling the broadhead bolts at Uncle Shi's chest and the crowd of pale villagers!</p>
<p>Under the dark eaves of the smithy shed twenty paces away, Lu Chen's right foot shifted half an inch forward on the frozen cinders, his hand inches from the forty-pound iron striker's sledge.</p>
<p>Within the absolute, glacial calm of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>, every detail of the square was etched in frozen relief: - Zhao Kuan standing with his hand resting on the ninety-jin iron whip-rod, his <strong>Early Tier Four (Slag-Bone Calcining)</strong> skeleton carrying four thousand jin of force; - Five Tier-Two and Tier-Three mounted guards on armored Crag-Mules, two with cocked iron crossbows aimed directly at Uncle Shi and young Shi Tou at ten paces; - And Lu Chen's own body—barely two days into <strong>Early Tier One (Ash-Skin Scouring)</strong>, with nine hundred and fifty jin of strength and uncalcined mortal bones.</p>
<p>If Lu Chen charged out of the smithy shed right now with the forty-pound sledge, he could maybe crush the skull of the nearest Tier-Two guard by surprise. On the second breath, the two cocked crossbows would punch through Uncle Shi and Shi Tou's chests, Zhao Kuan's ninety-jin iron whip-rod would shatter Lu Chen's uncalcined spine in three exchanges, and by nightfall the Black-Rock Garrison would ride up the gorge and burn all seventy-three people of Ash-Brook Village to cinders for "bandit rebellion."</p>
<p>Lying against the wheel of the tribute sledge with blood bubbling on his lips and his right collarbone snapped clean through, Old Tie Shan saw Lu Chen's boots shift in the shadow of the smithy shed.</p>
<p>With a desperate, agonizing wheeze, the one-armed old man twisted his head toward the smithy and glared directly at Lu Chen with his single bloodshot eye—giving a tiny, fierce shake of his head:</p>
<p><em>Don't move! If you die here, Tie Niu dies in the dark, and the village dies with us!</em></p>
<p>Lu Chen's fingers closed around the ash-wood handle of the forty-pound sledge so hard that the seasoned wood cracked lengthwise with a faint <em>creak</em>—and then, slowly, deliberately, he lowered his heel back to the cinders.</p>
<p>Blood seeped from the corner of Lu Chen's lower lip where his teeth had bitten through the flesh. He swallowed the warm, salty blood straight down into his stomach without wiping his mouth, burning the face of <strong>Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan</strong>, the name of <strong>Pit Overseer Luo Shan</strong>, and the deadline of the <strong>twentieth day of the twelfth month</strong> into the very marrow of his soul.</p>
<p>In the square, seeing that no villager dared raise a hand against the aimed crossbows, Zhao Kuan snorted, signaled his guards to hitch the two loaded tribute sledges behind their pack-mules, and wheeled his Crag-Mule toward the southern gate.</p>
<p>"Eight hundred jin of Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron, or thirty taels of Prefectural Silver by the twentieth of the twelfth month!" Zhao Kuan called back with a mocking laugh as the column trotted out through the palisade. "Start digging, Ash-Brook—maybe your one-armed smith can hammer iron with his teeth!"</p>
<p>As soon as the iron hoofbeats faded down the gorge, the villagers rushed forward. Granny Liu and Uncle Shi carefully lifted Old Tie Shan onto a plank stretcher and carried him into the smithy lean-to.</p>
<p>"Clean break of the right collarbone, and two cracked upper ribs," Granny Liu said grimly after setting the bone with wooden splints and binding Old Tie's only remaining arm tightly against his chest with hemp bandages. "He won't die, thank the Mountain God—his old bones are tough from forty years at the anvil. But he won't be able to lift a hammer or even pull the tongs for at least two months."</p>
<p>On the straw pallet, Old Tie Shan stared up at the soot-blackened thatch roof, silent tears finally leaking from his clouded left eye and his good right eye into his singed gray beard.</p>
<p>"Two months..." the old man whispered, his voice broken. "The twentieth of the twelfth month is sixty-five days away. Eight hundred jin of Grade-One Cold-Slag Iron... or thirty taels of silver. With my right arm strapped to my chest, I can't even hold the guide-hammer. My boy... my Tie Niu is going to drown in the dark in Drift Four..."</p>
<p>Inside the lean-to, Uncle Shi bowed his grizzled head in helpless grief, while Shi Tou knelt by the door punching the frozen dirt until his knuckles bled.</p>
<p>Lu Chen stepped quietly to the side of the straw pallet.</p>
<p>He knelt on one knee beside Old Tie Shan, took the broken half of the iron mine-tally stamped with the character <strong>"Seven"</strong> that had slipped from the old man's apron, and placed it firmly back over Old Tie's heart.</p>
<p>"Old Tie," Lu Chen said, his quiet voice ringing with a steadiness that made Uncle Shi, Granny Liu, and Shi Tou all look up at once. "Your eyes aren't broken, and my two hands aren't broken. You sit on the stool by the hearth and call the heats. I will mine the ore, I will burn the charcoal, I will hunt the ridge beasts for the silver, and I will swing both hammers myself. In sixty-five days, we are walking into Black-Slag Canyon with the iron on our sledges—and bringing Tie Niu home."</p>
