<p>Seven heats became seventy. One day at the anvil blurred into seven.</p>
<p>Time in Ash-Brook Village was not measured by incense clocks or sect bells, but by the shrinking pile of black-slag ore beside the shed door, the growing stack of heavy grey mattock-heads along the cooling wall, and the number of times the skin on Lu Chen's palms tore open, scabbed over with black herb-crust, and peeled away again.</p>
<p>Every morning before the grey light touched the Black-Briar Barren Ridge, Lu Chen woke on his straw pallet with his joints stiff as rusted hinges. Before touching his bowl of millet gruel, he stepped barefoot into the snow behind the lean-to, picked up the forty-pound striker's sledge by the very end of its handle, and practiced three hundred slow, waist-driven lever thrusts into the freezing wind—rebuilding the neuromuscular pathways between his shattered mortal spine and the weapon in his hands.</p>
<p>From dawn until noon, he swung the sledge across the anvil opposite Old Tie Shan, driving the spiral cold-shatter slag out of billet after billet of Black-Marrow iron.</p>
<p>At midday, when the furnace had to be banked for an hour to clear the clinker-slag from the air pipe, Lu Chen climbed the slick shale switchbacks of Frost-Tooth Gully with fourteen-year-old <strong>Shi Tou</strong> to pry another basket of ore from the cliff, or walked down to the creek with <strong>Uncle Shi</strong> to haul water-logged iron-pine logs for the charcoal pit.</p>
<p>At night, after a supper of coarse millet and whatever tough crow or hare meat Uncle Shi's snares had caught, <strong>Granny Liu</strong> came with her bubbling clay jar of <strong>Blood-Briar and Iron-Beetle Paste</strong>.</p>
<p>By the fourth night, the first layer of burnt, dead mortal skin across Lu Chen's palms, forearms, and shoulders began to slough off in stiff, translucent sheets like shed snakeskin. Underneath was raw, tender crimson flesh that bled at the slightest brush of rough hemp—yet when Granny Liu slapped the second coat of scalding paste directly onto that raw flesh, and Lu Chen went back to swinging the forty-pound sledge the next morning in front of the thousand-degree forge, something miraculous began to happen inside his dermal tissue.</p>
<p>In the Great Desolate Origin Universe, the human body possessed a dormant evolutionary resilience born from generations of struggling against the harsh <strong>Origin-Grain</strong>. Under the alternating extremes of searing forge radiation, bone-jarring mechanical percussion from the anvil, and the iron-beetle chitin in Granny Liu's paste, the regenerating skin cells did not grow back soft and porous. Instead, the collagen fibers cross-linked in dense, overlapping lattice-weaves—drinking in trace amounts of refined iron-ash and expelling the stagnant water of mortal bloat.</p>
<p>On the evening of the twelfth day, as Lu Chen finished punching the eye-sockets into the twenty-eighth and final tribute mattock-head, a red-hot splinter of slag-scale flew off the anvil at the speed of a crossbow bolt and struck the bare back of his left forearm.</p>
<p><em>Tink!</em></p>
<p>Instead of slicing into his flesh and drawing a line of blood as it would have a week ago, the jagged red-hot splinter bounced off the dull, copper-gray skin of his forearm, leaving only a tiny white chalk-mark that faded without even raising a blister!</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan, who had been reaching for the brine ladle to douse the burn, stopped with his hand in midair. He reached out with his thumb and pressed hard against the skin of Lu Chen's forearm and the thick, seamless pad of dark-gold callus across Lu Chen's palm.</p>
<p>The skin felt cool, dense, and unyielding—like cured bull-neck leather boiled in oil and iron-water.</p>
<p>"The third peeling isn't even finished," Old Tie Shan breathed, his single eye wide in the firelight, "and the slag-sparks are already bouncing off your forearm! Boy, you're half a step away from <strong>Early Tier One of the Mortal-Shedding Stage—Ash-Skin Scouring</strong>! Most village hunters take two full winters of rubbing ash-paste and hauling rock just to toughen their hands, and your whole upper frame is already turning to ox-hide in twelve days!"</p>
<p>Lu Chen looked down at the faint white mark on his forearm, flexing his fingers around the sledgehammer shaft.</p>
<p>Compared to his former body that could withstand the explosion of stars, a layer of toughened mortal skin that could deflect a stray forge-spark was laughably humble. Yet for the first time since falling through the Dimensional Slag-Trench, when he drew a deep breath of the freezing night air, the gritty <strong>Desolate Ash-Qi</strong> no longer scraped raw blood from his pores. His body had laid down its very first brick on the thousand-mile road back to the heavens.</p>
