<p><em>TUG!</em></p>
<p>Warden Ma Gui yanked backward on his whip handle with all one thousand eight hundred jin of his <strong>Early Tier Two</strong> strength—and the whip didn't budge a tenth of an inch! It felt as if the end of his lash had been welded into the solid granite mountain!</p>
<p>Before Ma Gui could even draw the short iron club from his belt, Lu Chen gave a gentle, two-inch flick of his wrist.</p>
<p>A wave of <strong>Tier Three Bowstring-Sinew</strong> vibration shot back down the twelve-foot leather lash like a live bolt of lightning, shattering the wooden whip-handle into splinters inside Ma Gui's palm and blasting the Early Tier Two warden ten paces backward on his rump into the scalding sulfur-brine with his right wrist dislocated!</p>
<p>"Who—?! Guarantor?!" Ma Gui scrambled backward in the water, clutching his swollen wrist in terror as he saw the seven-foot-two-inch, eighty-jin solid black-iron spear in the stranger's other hand and remembered Warden He Da's warning at the landing: <em>That's the Tier-Three monster who split the town test-block with a bare iron bar—don't provoke him alone before Boss Luo's twentieth-day trap is ready!</em></p>
<p>Clutching his dislocated wrist, Warden Ma Gui scurried thirty yards back up the drift toward the guard post without daring to look back.</p>
<p>At the dead-end rock face, twenty-two-year-old <strong>Tie Niu</strong> leaned heavily against his pickaxe handle on one good leg, squinting through sweat-stung, sunken eyes at the tall young man in the patched hemp shirt who had just caught an iron-wired whip barehanded.</p>
<p>"Who... who are you, brother?" Tie Niu croaked, his voice cracked like dry leather from months of breathing sulfur steam. "Why did you come down into Drift Four? Nobody comes into a quarantined shaft unless—"</p>
<p>Lu Chen didn't speak right away.</p>
<p>He grounded the butt of his eighty-jin spear in the gravel, reached inside his tunic with his right hand, and pulled out two things: First, a cloth-wrapped package of <strong>fire-dried Scarred King-Boar Loin and Roasted Iron-Husk Millet Cakes</strong> that <strong>Widow Qin</strong> and <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong> had packed over the Ash-Brook smithy hearth; And second, on its braided horsehair cord, the broken upper half of the stamped iron mine-tally marked with the character <strong>"Seven"</strong>!</p>
<p>With trembling, soot-blackened fingers, Tie Niu reached inside the waistband of his own ragged loincloth and pulled out the matching lower half of the broken iron tally that he had kept tied against his skin through one thousand days and nights of hell in Pit #7.</p>
<p>Lu Chen stepped forward and pressed the two jagged iron halves together in Tie Niu's palm.</p>
<p><em>CLINK.</em></p>
<p>The three-year-old fracture line matched without a hair's gap—forming the complete iron tally of <strong>Ash-Brook Village</strong>.</p>
<p>"My name is Lu Chen," Lu Chen said softly, his calm, warm voice cutting through the hiss of the sulfur vents as he supported Tie Niu's elbow so the crippled young man wouldn't have to bear the fifteenfold gravity on his crushed ankle. "A month ago, your father <strong>Old Tie Shan</strong> and <strong>Uncle Shi</strong> pulled me half-dead out of Frost-Tooth Gully and gave me a pallet by the forge hearth. Your father's right arm is whole and strong; all eight hundred and twelve jin of <strong>Grade-One Nine-Folded Cold-Slag Iron</strong> have been weighed and stamped at the County Scales; and your father is sitting right up at the top of the winch-shaft waiting to take you home."</p>
<p>For five full seconds, twenty-two-year-old Tie Niu—who had taken three hundred whip-lashes and a crushed ankle without shedding a single tear in front of the pit-wardens—stared down at the complete iron tally and the fragrant boar-meat from his father's hearth.</p>
<p>Then the big, broad-shouldered young blacksmith folded forward, pressed his forehead against Lu Chen's shoulder, and wept silently—great, shuddering, soundless sobs that shook his starved frame while hot tears washed two clean tracks down his coal-blackened cheeks.</p>
<p>Fifty feet back down the dim tunnel, thirty-nine other skeletal debt-miners stopped swinging their pickaxes and looked toward the end of the drift, a spark of forgotten human light flickering for the first time in their dead eyes.</p>
