<p>The plank door of the lean-to scraped open against the packed-earth floor, letting in a gust of sleet-filled wind and the sharp <em>tink-tink</em> of cooling slag from an outer shed.</p>
<p>Two men ducked beneath the low stone lintel, bringing the smell of woodsmoke and wet sheepskin into the narrow space.</p>
<p>The first was a broad-shouldered, heavily stooped man near sixty wearing a scorched ox-hide apron over a patched hemp tunic. His left sleeve was folded flat and pinned to his shoulder with a bent iron nail; his remaining right arm was thick as a char-oak branch, crisscrossed with white slag-burn scars and knotted blue veins. His beard was gray and singed short on one side from years of leaning too close to a forge fire.</p>
<p>Behind him stepped a lean, weather-beaten hunter in a greased boar-skin coat, a bone-handled skinning knife at his belt and three fingers missing from his left hand. Between his remaining thumb and index finger and his right palm, he balanced a chipped earthenware bowl steaming with pitch-black liquid.</p>
<p>"Look at the straw by his shoulder, Old Tie," the three-fingered hunter muttered in a gravelly frontier dialect, nodding toward the pallet. "He moved his arm. Granny Liu said anyone washed down Frost-Tooth Gully in the black-sleet storm with lungs full of ash-water would stiffen before the third cock-crow."</p>
<p>The one-armed blacksmith—<strong>Old Tie Shan</strong>—squatted beside the straw with a stiff grunt of his knees. His left eye was slightly clouded by a forge-cataract, but his right eye was sharp and steady as he looked down at Lu Chen's pale face and the rusted iron ring clenched in Lu Chen's bruised left fist.</p>
<p>"His eyes are awake," Old Tie Shan said, his voice rough from decades of coal smoke. "No fever-madness in them either. Boy, can you understand my tongue?"</p>
<p>When the Heaven-Shackling Harvester Barge's chains had slammed into Lu Chen's soul above the Primordial Wall, the branding arrays of the Bailiff Court had dumped a torrent of frontier tally-script and common speech directly into his sea of consciousness. Though his throat felt as if it had been scoured with sand, Lu Chen parted his cracked lips and forced air past his vocal cords.</p>
<p>"I... understand," Lu Chen rasped, each syllable costing an ache behind his sternum. "This junior... is called Lu Chen. Thank you both... for picking my bones out of the gully."</p>
<p>The three-fingered hunter—<strong>Uncle Shi</strong>, leader of <strong>Ash-Brook Village's</strong> six-man hunting party—snorted softly, though the hard lines around his mouth eased a fraction. "Don't waste wind on polite city talk, kid. Out here under the <strong>Black-Briar Barren Ridge</strong>, nobody saves a stranger for charity. Old Tie's striker boy died of the black-lung last winter, and his own son <strong>Tie Niu</strong> was hauled off to the <strong>Black-Slag Canyon Pits</strong> three autumns ago. When my grandson <strong>Shi Tou</strong> spotted your rags caught on the thorn-bushes by the creek, Old Tie gave Granny Liu his last half-measure of salt and two iron arrowheads to boil this bowl of <strong>Ash-Root and Rock-Adder Gall</strong>. If you live, you pull his bellows until the winter iron-tax is forged."</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan reached down with his single scarred hand, slid his thick forearm beneath Lu Chen's neck with surprising gentleness, and tilted the chipped clay bowl to Lu Chen's lips. "Drink it while it's boiling hot, lad. If it cools, the snake-gall curdles and won't cut the ash-clots in your chest."</p>
<p>The stench rising from the bowl was enough to make a starving dog back away—a pungent brew of charred bitter-root, rusted iron-scale, and acrid green viper bile.</p>
<p>Through the three-pace clarity of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>, Lu Chen perceived the crude, violent nature of the medicine. In his previous life in the lower realms, even a first-stage mortal apothecary would have discarded such unrefined dregs as toxic, for the crushed Ash-Root was full of microscopic silicate needles that tore at the lining of the stomach. Yet here in the Great Desolate Origin Universe, where the invasive Desolate Ash-Qi clung to mortal blood like congealed grease, gentle herbs were useless; only these harsh, barbed mineral roots could violently scrape the petrifying ash out of a mortal's lungs before it hardened into stone.</p>
<p>Lu Chen did not flinch or turn his head. Bracing his trembling right hand against the dirt floor under the tenfold gravity, he opened his mouth and swallowed the scalding black sludge in four slow, steady gulps.</p>
<p>Liquid fire tore down his gullet. The caustic rock-adder bile hit his bruised stomach and surged into his chest veins, triggering violent muscle spasms that tried to fold his spine in half. Cold sweat burst from every pore on his forehead; a metallic surge of black, clotted blood rose from the bottom of his lungs.</p>
<p>Instead of coughing wildly and tearing his cracked ribs further apart, Lu Chen locked his jaw, sank his mind into the absolute calm of <em>Prism Stillness</em>, and contracted his diaphragm in three precise, measured pulses—expelling a single mouthful of tar-black, ash-laden blood neatly onto a clump of dry ash beside the pallet without spilling a drop of the medicine.</p>
<p>Then he leaned back against the mud wall, eyes half-closed, regulating his ragged breaths in a slow four-beat cadence as the scorching heat of the Ash-Root chewed through the frost in his limbs.</p>
<p>Uncle Shi stared at the black clot on the floor, then at the young man's calm, sweat-drenched face where not a single whimper had escaped.</p>
<p>"By the Ridge-God's teeth," Uncle Shi muttered under his breath, glancing at Old Tie Shan. "That adder-gall makes grown hunters howl and claw the dirt the first time it hits a cracked lung. Where did this kid crawl out of?"</p>
<p>Old Tie Shan took the empty bowl, wiping a drip from the rim with his thumb, his weathered face softening almost imperceptibly. "Doesn't matter where he crawled from. A man who can swallow bitter gall without crying can hold a hammer. Sleep until dawn, Lu Chen. There's half a bowl of millet gruel in the pot if you can walk to the hearth tomorrow."</p>
