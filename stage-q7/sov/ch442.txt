<p>That night, four great iron cauldrons were set boiling over open log fires in the center of the village square.</p>
<p>Even though Uncle Shi strictly rationed the prime salted boar quarters so the two thousand jin of meat would keep all seventy-three villagers fed through the deep freeze of the twelfth and first months, the giant marrow-bones, wolf ribs, boar offal, and ten pounds of rich back-fat boiled with <strong>Iron-Husk Millet</strong> and dried wild garlic turned into forty gallons of thick, fragrant, golden-white bone soup.</p>
<p>For children who had eaten nothing but bitter acorn-chaff and turnip skins for three weeks, the first bowl of hot, fatty marrow soup was like liquid heaven. Even after their bellies were round and tight, the five- and six-year-olds sat around the cauldrons licking the grease from their wooden spoons until their cheeks shone in the firelight.</p>
<p>Shortly before midnight, after the cheering in the square had died down to contented murmurs and most of the villagers had gone back to their warm huts, Lu Chen sat alone on the split-log stool inside the smithy shed.</p>
<p>At his feet, the charcoal hearth glowed a soft, sleepy red. His eighty-jin solid black-iron boar-spear leaned against the stone anvil block, freshly wiped down with melted boar-tallow until its spiral-grained shaft gleamed like dark obsidian.</p>
<p>Having broken into <strong>Early Tier Two (Iron-Flesh Compacting)</strong> at Split-Stone Defile the morning before, Lu Chen had spent the last two hours simmering the <strong>Silver-Backed Alpha Wolf's Spine-Marrow</strong> with <strong>Blood-Moss</strong>, drinking half the pot himself and setting the other half aside in a covered clay jar on the hearth-stones for <strong>Uncle Shi</strong>. Now, as the dense Tier-Two wolf-marrow circulated through his compacted muscles, a steady, soothing warmth radiated from his calves up through his thighs, back, and shoulders, consolidating his strength firmly at <strong>two thousand one hundred and fifty jin</strong>.</p>
<p>From the pile of seasoned <strong>Iron-Pine</strong> kindling beside the hearth, Lu Chen picked up a six-inch heartwood splinter—dense, reddish-brown wood that sank like stone in water—and drew a small, narrow-bladed bone-carving knife he had forged from a scrap of chisel steel.</p>
<p>Sinking his mind into the quiet clarity of <strong>Prism Stillness</strong>, he began to whittle the hard iron-pine heartwood in the orange firelight.</p>
<p>His calloused thumbs—now tough enough to turn a saber edge, yet capable of one-thousandth-of-an-inch precision from two lifetimes of antiquities restoration—guided the tiny blade along the tight grain of the wood. Tiny curls of fragrant red pine-shavings drifted down onto his boots.</p>
<p>Under his knife, a slender, five-inch wooden hairpin gradually emerged, crowned at its head with a cluster of four-petaled <strong>Osmanthus Blossoms</strong> so lifelike that each tiny petal curved as if catching a warm autumn breeze.</p>
<p>Looking down at the carved wooden osmanthus blossoms in his palm, Lu Chen's dark eyes grew distant.</p>
<p>Three hundred years ago—and yet only twenty-eight days ago by the count of his bleeding hands in this new world—an osmanthus tree ten thousand years old had bloomed above a cedar table where his mother Meng Wan ladled longevity noodles, where his father Lu Shan laughed over a jar of wine, and where Su Qingxue and Su Wan'er sat side by side waiting for him to come down from the watch-cliff. Right now, they were still sitting at that table inside the <strong>Nine-Epoch Inverse Chrono-Cocoon</strong>, frozen between one heartbeat and the next in the cold dark of Sector Nine-Four-Seven.</p>
<p><em>Tap... tap... tap.</em></p>
<p>The soft sound of a willow switch on the frozen cinders outside the shed brought Lu Chen gently back from the starry void.</p>
<p>He looked up to see <strong>Widow Qin</strong> and little blind <strong>Xiao Ya</strong> standing at the edge of the hearth-light, carrying a clean wooden basin of warm snow-water and fresh linen bandages to change the dressing on his left forearm where the cauterized hyena-fang burn was healing.</p>
<p>As Widow Qin knelt silently beside the stool and unwrapped the old bandage—revealing that under Lu Chen's <strong>Early Tier Two</strong> vitality, the seared scar had already shed its scab, leaving only four faint silver marks on his bronze skin—Lu Chen reached out and gently slid the carved <strong>Iron-Pine Osmanthus Hairpin</strong> into the frayed hemp braid holding Widow Qin's headscarf, and placed a smaller wooden <strong>Nine-Petaled Lotus Charm</strong> on a red string into little Xiao Ya's palm.</p>
<p>Little Xiao Ya traced the nine tiny wooden petals with her fingertips, her face lighting up with a pure, radiant smile as she smelled the sweet pine-resin scent of the carved wood.</p>
<p>Widow Qin touched the carved osmanthus hairpin in her hair. For a woman who had worn nothing but mourning rags and coal-dust since her husband died saving Uncle Shi two winters ago, her rough fingers trembled as they traced the delicate four-petaled blossoms. Two hot tears spilled silently down her chapped cheeks; she bowed her head deeply to Lu Chen, her hands signing two simple words in the firelight:</p>
<p><em>Little Brother.</em></p>
