<p>When Lu Chen and Shi Tou trudged back through the log gate of Ash-Brook Village just as the afternoon snow-squall blinded the ridge, the sight of two baskets brimming with oily blue-black <strong>Black-Marrow Core Ore</strong>—topped by a fat, dead Iron-Beak Ridge Crow—drew half a dozen startled villagers out from under their eaves.</p>
<p>At the smithy shed, Old Tie Shan picked up one of the blue-black nodules with his single scarred hand, hefted its dense weight, and let out a long breath that misted white in the cold.</p>
<p>"Sixty jin of pure marrow-heart," the old blacksmith muttered, his cloudy eye glinting in the forge light. "With this, we won't just pass Sub-Bailiff Zhao Kuan's inspection on the fifteenth—we'll have enough spare iron left over to shoe the village tribute sledges before the deep frost locks the pass."</p>
<p>Yet when Old Tie Shan looked from the ore basket to Lu Chen's hands and shoulders, his rough expression darkened.</p>
<p>The rawhide straps of the pack-basket, multiplied by twelvefold gravity on the steep descent down Frost-Tooth Gully, had sawed straight through the shoulders of Lu Chen's hemp shirt and dug two purple, bleeding grooves across his collarbones. His linen hand-wraps were stiff with frozen red mud and blood where the thirty-pound pry-bar had rubbed the first layer of new calluses completely off.</p>
<p>"Sit down on the anvil block and don't move," Old Tie Shan barked. Turning toward the stone hut next door, where curls of acrid green herb-smoke drifted from a clay chimney, he bellowed, "Granny Liu! Bring the black jar and a scoop of hot lye-ash! The boy's ready for his first <strong>Skin-Scouring Rub</strong>!"</p>
<p>Two minutes later, <strong>Granny Liu</strong> hobbled into the smithy shed leaning on a gnarly ironwood cane. She was past seventy, her back bent nearly double by decades of frontier gravity and her eyes squinted behind thick folds of wrinkled skin, yet her gnarled fingers were steady as iron pincers. Clutched against her apron was a heavy glazed-clay jar smelling of fermented <strong>Blood-Briar Sap</strong>, charred <strong>Iron-Beetle Shells</strong>, and caustic wood-lye.</p>
<p>"Take off the shirt, lad," Granny Liu wheezed, setting the clay jar beside the glowing forge hearth so the paste inside began to bubble softly. "If you want to swing Old Tie's forty-pound striker-sledge tomorrow without the skin peeling off your ribs and palms like boiled onion-peel, we have to scour the soft mortal dead-flesh off your frame tonight. I won't lie to you—three winters ago, when <strong>Tie Niu</strong> took his first scouring rub at sixteen, he bit clean through a willow gag and passed out before I finished his back."</p>
<p>Lu Chen untied his waist-cord and peeled the blood-stiffened hemp shirt over his head.</p>
<p>In the orange glow of the charcoal hearth, his lean, broad-shouldered torso looked like a piece of cracked porcelain held together by iron staples: pale from his days of near-death, bruised black across the left ribs, and scored with red strap-burns over the shoulders.</p>
<p>Granny Liu dipped a coarse brush made of stiff boar-bristles into the bubbling black jar and slapped the scalding paste straight across Lu Chen's raw shoulders, chest, forearms, and bleeding palms.</p>
<p><em>HSSSSSSSS—!</em></p>
<p>It felt as though someone had poured molten lead mixed with ground glass and vinegar directly onto open wounds.</p>
<p>The caustic <strong>Blood-Briar and Iron-Beetle Paste</strong> was the frontier's crudest, most brutal doorway into <strong>Tier 1 of the Mortal-Shedding Stage (Ash-Skin Scouring)</strong>. In the Great Desolate Origin Universe, a mortal's natural skin was too porous to keep out the corrosive Desolate Ash-Qi; the paste violently burned away the fragile outer epidermis while driving pulverized iron-beetle chitin and blood-briar tannins into the deeper dermal layer, forcing the body to regenerate a dense, fibrous hide tough as cured ox-leather.</p>
<p>Every nerve from Lu Chen's fingertips to the base of his skull screamed in white-hot agony. The veins in his neck stood out like knotted cords; his teeth clenched so hard a drop of blood seeped from his gums.</p>
<p>"Here, boy—bite the wood!" Old Tie Shan stepped forward, trying to shove a thick chunk of peeled willow branch between Lu Chen's teeth.</p>
<p>Lu Chen shook his head once, barely a quarter-inch, his eyes fixed on the white-hot core of the charcoal forge.</p>
<p>Inside his mind, <strong>Prism Stillness</strong> held his consciousness like an anchor dropped into a frozen abyss. Whenever the searing fire of the paste threatened to make his torso convulse—which would tear his newly knitted ribs—he thought of the frozen courtyard of Warm-Spring Valley. He thought of his father Lu Shan's hand frozen mid-reach, his mother's tear suspended in gray time-crystal, and the ten-thousand-year countdown ticking silently in the dark.</p>
<p><em>Compared to losing them,</em> Lu Chen thought in the dead silence of his sea of consciousness, <em>this is only warm mud.</em></p>
<p>For two full incense sticks of time, Granny Liu scrubbed every inch of his upper body and hands with the stiff boar-bristle brush until his skin turned a terrifying, blistered charcoal-black. Not once did Lu Chen groan, flinch, or pull his hands back by a hair's breadth.</p>
<p>When Granny Liu finally dropped the brush into the bucket with trembling fingers and wiped her forehead, she looked up at the silent young man sitting straight-backed on the anvil block and crossed herself with the sign of the Mountain God.</p>
<p>"Old Tie," Granny Liu whispered, her voice shaking slightly, "I've scoured forty boys in Ash-Brook over fifty winters. I have never seen a heart made of harder iron than this one."</p>
<p>Lu Chen exhaled a long, thin plume of white steam into the freezing air of the shed. Looking down past his blackened, stinging palms toward the corner of the hearth, his eyes rested on the long ash-wood handle of the <strong>forty-pound iron striker-sledge</strong> leaning against the wall.</p>
<p>Tomorrow, the real hammering would begin.</p>
